## 1. The claim

**An approximate posterior agrees with the exact sampler's on M0, at a fraction of
the cost. On the reduced syn3A model it is calibrated, and it is efficient: it reaches
the linearised information bound for D11's six.** It has two factors:
- an exact likelihood for the single-cell transcripts;
- a Gaussian likelihood for the bulk metabolites, centred on a mean-field
  description of gene expression.

**The method is a formalism swap.**
- **One gene-expression interface, two descriptions:**
  - the exact stochastic (jump) modules;
  - mean-field ODE modules with the same states and coupling edges.
- **Each description gets its matching inference:**
  - the exact three-block Gibbs sampler for the jump modules;
  - the mixed likelihood, sampled by NUTS, for the mean field.
- **The choice is made per data stream.** Transcripts keep their exact discrete
  likelihood. Bulk metabolites use the mean field.

**Its size.**
- The claim is stated for three routes only: exact Gibbs, the mixed likelihood and
  plain NUTS. It is not stated for any composition.
- Every validation dataset is generated by the full two-way model. The mean field is
  an inference approximation, never the reference (decision A2).

## 3. Scientific validity

### The target

For θ = (θ_G, θ_M), with gene-expression parameters θ_G and metabolic parameters θ_M,
and data D = (D_tx, D_met):

```
p̃(θ | D) ∝ p(θ) · Π_cells Π_g L_tx,g(θ_G; k̄_tx(θ)) · N(log D_met; m(θ), σ_b² I + S)
```

- **`L_tx,g`** is gene g's exact transcript likelihood (B1), at rate constants
  `k̄_tx(θ)` rebuilt from the mean-field pools.
- **`m(θ)`** is `log x̄` from the mean-field composition: B2's modules coupled to the
  metabolism through route (ii)'s edges (B4).
- **`S`** is the covariance of `log x̄_C`, the C-cell mean, about `m`. It comes from an
  ensemble (§4, D-S).
- **σ_b** is sampled, as in V7.

**What is exact, and what is not.**
- The gene-expression network is first order: transcription is zeroth order, and
  decay, translation and translocation are first order. So given the rate constants,
  the mean equations close exactly as linear ODEs.
- The approximation enters in three places only. Each is declared as a reduction:

| Reduction | What it drops | Measured by |
|---|---|---|
| **The Jensen gap** | The metabolism is nonlinear. Feeding it mean enzyme counts and mean drains is not the mean of feeding it each cell's. The drain's clip at zero adds to it | B3 (AV5) |
| **Continuous drains** | The 1 s deferred-counter drain becomes a continuous flux in the ODE path | B4, against the hybrid driver at pinned paths |
| **Mean-field feedback** | The rate constants are rebuilt from the population-mean pools under the current θ, so cell-to-cell fluctuations in the feedback are dropped. This applies to the bulk factor and to the transcript factor's `k̄_tx` | B3 (AV5), with A3's frozen-pool arm as a bound |

### The rate-constant clock

B1 and B2 share it, and it is the driver's own:
- `handshake_step!` runs the exchange, including any rebuild, before the jump step.
  So window m's constants are:
  - `R_{m−1}` on [60(m−1), 60m − 1);
  - `R_m` on [60m − 1, 60m).
- This is the clock `src/csmc.jl:11-34` is written for, and V1 tests it.
- **The mean field rebuilds with the same discrete 60 s step,** never a continuous
  k(pool(t)). Otherwise B1's factor would be exact against a clock that B2 does not
  run.

Per gene g and window m, with births `k` and deaths `μ_g m`, where
μ_g = krnadeg / len_g:

```
L_tx,g,m = [ P_{k_{g,m−1}, μ_g}(59) · P_{k_{g,m}, μ_g}(1) ] (y_{m−1}, y_m)
```

### Assumptions, each with its regime

| Assumption | Holds when | What breaks outside it |
|---|---|---|
| The transcript chain is truncated harmlessly at its cap | `truncation_mass` < 1e-12 at the largest birth rate the prior allows (the turnover ceiling) | The factor loses mass at large θ. Checked once per dataset (AV1) |
| The bulk factor is Gaussian in `log x̄` | Pools stay well above the one-particle floor and away from a clip | Pi, 3PG and 2PG sit near the floor on M0 (A1). §9 asks whether to keep them |
| S can be held fixed across θ | S changes slowly over the posterior's support | A fixed S mis-weights the bulk factor away from the reference θ. KS |
| Cells share the mean-field rate constants in the transcript factor | Rate-constant elasticities to the pools are small (0.04–0.09) | At a desaturated truth (A3's truth 2) per-cell feedback matters. AV5 sees its effect on the pools, and 5.2 sees it on the posterior |

## 4. Approach

**D-S: what S is. Proposed:** estimated once per dataset, at a reference θ, from a
forward ensemble of the full two-way model, and held fixed across θ.
- It is the sample covariance of `log x̄_C` over ensemble replicates of C cells,
  kept low rank: the leading eigenvectors above a variance floor, with the rest in
  the diagonal.
- **The reference θ:** the posterior mode under S = 0, found by optimisation, or
  the prior median if that fails. Recorded per dataset.
- **It is a declared reduction**, and KS measures its cost.
- **Re-estimating S per θ is ruled out.** It means an ensemble per log-density
  evaluation, about 1.8 CPU-h on Core A′, and no gradients. That kills NUTS.
- **The alternative is the linear noise approximation.** S per θ, differentiable,
  exact for the gene-expression block, linearised through the metabolism. It is §9's
  open question. It is the right next step if KS fires.

**Order and gates:**
1. **B1 and B2** are standalone and unit tested: AV1–AV4.
2. **B3** prototypes the coupled mean field inside the hybrid driver, as a
   throwaway: a reaction-free jump module holds the mean counts, the rounding is
   continuous, and the mean ODE is advanced in the script. **AV5 gates B4.**
3. **B4** is route (ii). The ODE path learns three edges:
   - catalytic edges read the mean protein states directly, so
     `_refuse_inert_catalytic` passes when an ODE module owns the count;
   - drains become continuous fluxes;
   - the rebuild becomes a 60 s discrete callback reading the ODE's own pools.

   Each is a declared reduction.
4. **B5:** the mixed likelihood and NUTS (Turing, or AdvancedHMC directly).
5. **B6:** the dispatcher, for the three routes only.
6. **B7–B9:** paper sections 5.2–5.4.

## 8. Kill criteria

- **KG — the Jensen gap is large (AV5).** The statistic is A3's, so the two are
  comparable:
  - `Σ_t (log x̄_MF − log x̄_SSA)² / (2σ_b²)` per pool and in total, at σ_b = 10%;
  - `x̄_SSA` is the 200-cell ensemble mean of the full two-way model at the same θ;
  - the noise is that sum's expected value with no shift, from the ensemble's split
    halves.
  - **Pass** at a truth if (agreed 2026-10-20):
    - the total is ≤ the noise;
    - no pool is above 3× its own noise.
  - **If it fails at truth 2 but passes elsewhere:** mean field is the wrong second
    description where ptsG is low. Stop and rethink before B4 (discussion note §14,
    step 4). Do not tune σ_b to pass.
- **KS — a fixed S is too crude.** On M0, 5.2's posterior with S fixed differs from
  one with S re-estimated at the posterior mean by more than the Monte Carlo floor.
  The response is the linear noise approximation (§9).
- **KC — no cost advantage.** On M0 an approximate posterior costs more than a tenth
  of F10's production posterior. The method then has no reason to exist at that
  rung.
