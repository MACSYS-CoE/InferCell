## What this says

One seed, one path and one node, so these are the measurements to plan from, not
a decision. Changing the integrator is spec task 1.6's decision and needs a §12
entry; it comes after V7, which belongs to the current integrator.

**Speed.** With KLU and the coloured sparse Jacobian, FBDF is 3.4× Rodas5P on both
models, and 3.1 to 3.9× with 16 copies running at once. Rodas5P with KLU is 1.6
to 1.8× serially and 2.0× at 16 copies. Both allocate almost nothing (1 to 7 KiB
per handshake against 1.5 to 1.8 MB), so their GC share is about 0 and they
barely slow down when packed onto a node (1.1 to 1.4×, against production's
1.3×). FBDF's gain is Jacobian reuse: about one Jacobian per handshake against
36 to 55. Swapped in out of place it is 1.6× *slower*, and loses half its time
to GC at 16 copies.

**The in-place dense variants collapse when packed** (4.5 to 5.6× slower at 16
copies, with little GC). Not diagnosed. A likely cause is the dense LU's BLAS
threads oversubscribing the cores, which KLU does not use. It does not matter
for the recommendation, since the KLU rows dominate them.

**Accuracy.** Over M0's 600 s, every Rodas5P variant agrees with the reference to
1e-5 particles. FBDF is off by up to about 1 particle, by more than half a
particle at 44 to 67 of the 600 handshakes. Over the full cycle even production
Rodas5P is off by up to 1.25 particles, at 535 of 6,300 handshakes: once the
clamped drain and the rounded exchange are in play, the pinned tolerance fixes
the trajectory only to about a particle. FBDF is about twice that (2.2 to 2.3
particles, about 1,180 handshakes). The clipped-drain count is 96 for every
variant. On corea, the Rodas5P KLU and in-place variants give production's
numbers to the reported precision. On M0 they differ from production in the
second significant figure of the error (1.0e-5 to 1.15e-5 against 1.08e-5).

**So:**
- **Rodas5P with KLU is the conservative change**: 1.6 to 2.0× faster, with
  production's accuracy and no allocation.
- **FBDF with KLU is the fast one**: 3.1 to 3.9×, at about one particle of
  extra disagreement, which is the size of the pinned tolerance's own disagreement
  over a full cycle.
- Either changes roundoff, so V2's bitwise replay and V4 to V5 would need
  rerunning.
- **Applied to the exact sampler's projected 60k to 150k CPU-h per full-scale
  posterior,** the KLU options would bring it to about 18k (FBDF, 3.4×) to 94k (Rodas5P, 1.6× serial; 75k at its 2.0× with 16 copies). That is still
  above the remaining budget, consistent with the note: the cost is the number of
  simulations.
