**Tightening the solver does not shrink these residuals, and it should not.**
Both moieties satisfy `nᵀf ≡ 0` exactly — every reaction's contribution
cancels term by term — so there is no local truncation error in them to
scale with the tolerance, and what the table shows is round-off, five orders
below the integrator bound in the last column. The two-rung ratios are not
the evidence and should not be read as one: over nine rungs the adenylate
residual wanders between 128 and 1,119 ulps of its sum with no trend, while
the evaluation count grows 36-fold, so any single pair of rungs gives an
arbitrary ratio between 0.2x and 1.8x.

This is the **second gate** of spec §3's exact-conservation exception (§12,
2026-09-11), not the first: these sums are not bitwise zero on the composed
right-hand side, because the terms arrive from three modules. The gate that
licenses the floor is the per-evaluation residual — one ulp of the conserved
sum — and `test/test_corea_nucleotide_recycling.jl` asserts it, together
with a six-rung ladder bounded by each rung's own `tol_C`. The evidence that
these checks can fail is that assertion and the mutation test, not this table.

### Pyrophosphatase removed

Pyrophosphate reaches **12.7902 mM** at 6,300 s from 0.1000 mM — a 128-fold
rise, against **0.369718 mM** in the configuration that keeps the enzyme.

**It strands the moiety; it does not diverge, and it cannot.** Phosphate is
closed in this composition at 35.0920 mM, and pyrophosphate ends holding
**72.9%** of it. With no route back to free phosphate the
substrate-level phosphorylation has nothing to work with, so ATP collapses
— below 1% of its initial value at t = 460.0 s, and 7.197e-21 mM at the end —
and charging stops with it, which is why the rise flattens (0.0000 mM
over the last save interval) rather than continuing.

The scoping note's **173 mM** is one pyrophosphate per charging event over a
whole cycle with nothing hydrolysing it. That is open-pool arithmetic: it
assumes charging runs at 553.1/s for 6,300 s, which needs a phosphate supply
the closed model does not have — Core A′'s own phosphate is a closed moiety,
which is what spec §3 check 4b asserts. Both numbers are recorded with the
model each belongs to. A stalled pathway is the stronger demonstration that
the enzyme is required, and it is the one that survives assembly.

## 8.7 — phosphate closure, both forms

| form | residual at (1e-10, 1e-8) | at a tenth of each | ratio |
|---|---|---|---|
| phosphate (exact) | 6.395e-14 mM | 3.837e-13 mM | 0.2x |
| phosphate (flux-corrected) | 3.340e-13 mM | 4.334e-13 mM | 0.8x |

Phosphate crossing the two inbound mass edges over the cycle: 0.3092 mM,
carried in from the resupplied 13DPG and PEP pools, one per turnover of the
GTP branch. **It is bounded because guanylate is**: nothing in Core A′ consumes
GTP until phase 9's translation, so PGK3 and PYK3 run until GDP is spent and
then stop — the crossing equals GDP₀ + GMP₀ = 0.3098 mM to 0.2%. That is a
structural statement about the composition. It was *not* true of a first
version of the double, which returned a zero derivative for the four
glycolytic pools and so did not hold them at all: `contributed_states` folds
recycling's terms into the owner's `du`, 13DPG fell 99.8% inside half a
second, and the branch then stopped on substrate rather than on guanylate,
at 0.0505 mM. The flux-corrected form subtracts the crossing; widening the
bound to swallow it instead would have hidden any real error of the same size.
The substrate-level phosphorylation crosses nothing — it turned over 345.15 mM
of phosphate over the cycle and took every one of them from the free pool,
which is what keeps the moiety closed and what a first version of the double
got wrong.

Both forms are linear invariants, so the ratio column carries the same
meaning it does above: tolerance-independence, not a five-fold fall.

## Wall-clock

One 6,300 s trajectory: **0.005 s** at the pinned tolerances, 0.007 s at
a tenth of each. 14 states and seven rate expressions, no handshake — this is
not Core A′'s cost and does not decide K1, which spec task 13.7 measures on
the assembled hybrid model.

It adds a third module measurement to §9's question of whether the full-cycle
checks belong in the
default suite, and the answer is that the *horizon* is free: at a few
milliseconds a trajectory, running 6,300 s rather than 600 s costs nothing,
so `test/test_corea_nucleotide_recycling.jl` asserts the phase's done-when at
the horizon the done-when names. What is not free is compilation — the two
full-cycle testsets add tens of seconds to the suite, almost all of it
Rodas5P specialising on new problem types, and shortening the horizon would
not recover any of it. The figure is not quoted here because it is a property
of the whole tree — phases 6 and 7 now compile the stiff path first, so the
marginal cost of this phase is smaller than it was measured to be alone. This driver is not a duplicate of those tests: it is where
the numbers are *recorded*, with the commit and job that produced them, so a
reader gets the measurements without running anything.

## What this does not cover

Every number here is the module's against *doubles*. `HeldGlycolytic` relaxes
four glycolytic pools to their registry values and rephosphorylates ADP at a
rate matched to the charging demand; phase 6's module replaces it with
reactions that do neither exactly, and in particular its pools are set by
glycolytic flux rather than held near a setpoint.
`ChargingDrain` is zeroth order in the tRNA pools where phase 9's module is
mass-action in both. The conservation and closure results are structural and
survive that replacement; the settled pyrophosphate concentration and the
crossing time do not, and are quoted as this composition's.
