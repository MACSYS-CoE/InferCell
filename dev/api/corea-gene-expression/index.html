Gene expression

The reduced syn3A model's three jump modules (transcription, transcript decay and translation), their mean field, and route (ii), which runs the whole model with gene expression swapped for that mean field. The overview lists the other pages.

Mean-field gene expression (track B, tasks B2 and B4; spec/phases/approximate-method.md). MeanField(members) turns a group of first-order :jump modules into one :ode module carrying their mean equations, d E[x]/dt = Σ_r a_r(E[x]) ν_r. It reads each propensity from the member's own closure and gets each state change by applying the member's own affect!, so the two descriptions cannot drift apart. It refuses a propensity that is not affine in the state, since the means are exact only then. gene_expression_mean_field(; genes) groups transcription, decay and translation, with the same states, parameters and coupling edges as the jump modules. With edges = :none it builds alone at pinned rate constants. Route (ii), build_mean_field_problem, runs the reduced model as one ODE with the jump modules swapped for one MeanField, and executes their coupling edges as part of the right-hand side or as callbacks; the plain build_problem does not.

InferCell.BASE_MAPPINGS — Constant

Which base count is charged against which nucleotide.

:corrected charges each base against the nucleotide it is actually polymerised from. :published reproduces MinCell_CMEODE.py:377-383, where the UTP term takes the cytosine count, the CTP term the guanine count and the GTP term the uracil count. The permutation moves each rate constant by about one percent, but it weights the GTP term by the uracil count rather than the guanine count, which makes each constant more sensitive to the live GTP pool than it should be — by that gene's U-to-G count ratio, a median 1.85× over the seventeen and spanning 1.53 to 2.45. GTP is one of the four reverse channels into this module; ATP, CTP and UTP enter the same rate law.

InferCell.transcription_rate_constant — Method
transcription_rate_constant(g, conc; base_mapping, rnap_conc) -> Float64

The published rate law of MinCell_CMEODE.py:351-391, equation 3:

kcat_mod = min(rnaPolKcat · S_g , 180)              S_g = ptnCount / 180
NMonoSum = K_d · Σ_x  N_x / [NTP_x]
k_g      = kcat_mod / ( (1 + K₀/[RNAP])·K_d²/(C₁C₂) + NMonoSum + n_g − 1 )

C₁ and C₂ are the concentrations of the nucleotides the transcript's first two bases are polymerised from. N_x is the count of the base charged against NTP x, which is where BASE_MAPPINGS enters and where the published code permutes three of four.

Written once, as a free function, so every test asserts the module's own arithmetic rather than a second copy of it.

InferCell.DECAY_COUNTERS — Constant

The five decay counters, the registry pool each one touches, and the direction.

ATP_mRNAdeg is the decay energy, one ATP per nucleotide, debited from ATP. Its products, ADP and phosphate, are credited one each per ATP actually paid (spec §11 task 13.10).

InferCell.monomer_closure — Method
monomer_closure(genes, n0, n_end, polymerised, returned) -> NamedTuple

The per-moiety residual of decay's own check (task 12.6):

polymerised_x + Σ_g n0_g·x_g − returned_x − Σ_g n_end_g·x_g

for each base x. Transcripts present at the start were never polymerised in the run, and transcripts present at the end have not yet been returned, so the stocks enter on both sides. Every term is an integer, so a closed composition gives exactly zero.

genes is the extract, the transcription side's view of each transcript. polymerised and returned are (A, C, G, U) named tuples of totals, read from transcription's monomer counters and decay's NMP counters.

Jump-only runs. Those counters are run totals only when nothing clears them. In a hybrid composition the hook clears every counter at each drain, so their end values are one interval's accrual and the residual here is meaningless.

InferCell.TL_AA_TYPES — Constant

The number of per-amino-acid charged-tRNA pools the lumped pool stands in for.

The published law sums n_aa · riboKd / [aa-tRNA] over twenty pools and adds riboKd² / [fMet-tRNA]². The reduced model has one lumped charged pool, and each of those twenty-one concentrations reads its per-amino-acid share, [M_trna_chg_c] / 20. That keeps the published law's structure: at the nominal 0.2 mM charged pool the share is 0.01 mM against upstream's 150 copies, 0.0074 mM. Substituting the whole pool for each per-amino-acid pool would move each term's concentration 27-fold, and with it the pool's elasticity, from about 0.09 to about 0.005, for no reason but the lumping. Ours, chosen 2026-09-24 (spec §12), and reduction_notes says so.

InferCell.TRANSLATION_COUNTERS — Constant

The seven deferred cost counters translation accrues, what each touches, and what fills it.

  • GTP_translat is the elongation energy, two GTP per residue (MinCell_CMEODE.py:1008-1010; upstream names it ATP_translat, and its hook drains GTP, in_out.py:199-218). Its GDP and phosphate are credited one each per GTP actually paid (spec §11 task 13.10). Two per residue, and residues exclude the stop, so this is two GTP per protein fewer than upstream's 2·len(aasequence). That is ours.
  • tRNA_translat is the charged tRNA consumed, one per residue. It debits the charged pool and credits the uncharged one, one counter with two edges. The hook credits what the debit actually paid, so a clipped debit cannot create tRNA (spec §11 task 11.6).
  • ATP_transloc is translocation's energy, int(len(aasequence)/10) ATP per ptsG inserted (MinCell_CMEODE.py:1012-1013), debited from ATP, with ADP and phosphate credited like GTP's products.
  • The four carrier counters credit one new carrier each to the unphosphorylated state PtsTransport owns (spec §12, 2026-09-23 C). The three cytosolic carriers are credited as they are translated; ptsG as it is translocated, since only a membrane ptsG is a carrier.

per says what one event adds: :twice_residues, :residues, :transloc_atp or :one. event says which event: :translation, or :translocation.

InferCell.CoreATranslation — Type
CoreATranslation(; genes, residues, counters, clip, smoothing, interval, chg_mM, edges,
                  rebuilt)

Seventeen translation reactions and one translocation.

mRNA_g → mRNA_g + P_g + 2r_g·GTP_translat + r_g·tRNA_translat [+ carrier credit]
Pcyto_ptsG → P_ptsG + int((r+1)/10)·ATP_transloc + ptsG_transloc

InferCell.translation_rate_constant — Method
translation_rate_constant(length, residues, chg_mM; ribo_conc) -> Float64

The published restart law (translation_rate_restart.py:46-115), equation 3, with the lumped pool's per-amino-acid share c = chg_mM / 20 standing in for each of the twenty-one charged-tRNA concentrations:

k = kcat_mod / ( (1 + K₀/[ribosome])·K_d²/c² + residues·K_d/c + residues )

residues is the published n_tot − 1, since n_tot counts the stop codon, and it is also Σ n_aa, since the stop is no amino acid. So the per-gene composition drops out of the lumped law, which needs only the length and the residue count.

The share is floored at one particle, at the registry's volume, as upstream floors each pool with max(1, count). Without it an exhausted charged pool would divide by zero. ribo_conc is frozen at the registry's volume, as upstream computes ribosomeConc once at the initial radius.

Written once, as a free function, so tests assert the module's own arithmetic.

InferCell.MeanField — Type
MeanField(members; edges = :declared, id = :MeanField)

One :ode module carrying the mean equations of the :jump modules members. It has the same states, in member order, and the same parameters. A member's counters become cumulative expected drains.

Every member input must be another member's state, so the group is closed. Each reaction's state change must not depend on the state, and each propensity must be affine in it. Both are checked here, since the means are exact only for such a network.

edges = :declared carries the members' coupling edges and rebuilt parameters over unchanged. :none drops them, for a standalone build at pinned rate constants. Route (ii), build_mean_field_problem, executes them; the plain build_problem does not.

build_mean_field_problem(models; tspan = (0.0, 100.0), interval = 1.0,
                         radius_nm = nothing, initial_surface_area_nm2 = nothing,
                         footprint_nm2 = nothing, complete = false) -> MeanFieldRoute

Route (ii) of the approximate method: models with its :jump modules swapped to their mean field (mean_field_swap), every coupling edge executed inside one ODE, as this file's header describes. interval is the reference's handshake, which sets the rebuild's switch time (k · interval_r − interval). The geometry keywords and complete are the hybrid's.

The plain build_problem on an all-ODE composition is unchanged, and still refuses catalytic edges.

Solve with solve_mean_field. The rebuilt, catalytic and geometric slots of prob.p are written by the route, not read from it (mean_field_written_params).

InferCell.mean_field_swap — Method
mean_field_swap(models) -> Vector{AbstractSubModel}

models with every :jump module replaced by one MeanField group holding all of them, coupling edges kept, placed after the :ode modules. A module with no reactions, such as HeldEnzymeCounts, joins the group and its counts are held. A composition with no :jump module is returned unchanged.

InferCell.solve_mean_field — Method
solve_mean_field(route; p = route.prob.p, saveat = [], solver = Rodas5P(),
                 abstol = 1e-10, reltol = 1e-8, maxiters = 10^5)
