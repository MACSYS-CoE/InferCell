# KIM-68: M5 prep — `predict`'s scheduler, the review's simplifications, and the threading diagnosis

## Context

KIM-68 is M5's first job. It comes before the reduced-model budget (KIM-42), and it
collects what the PR #123 review left optional:

- **Threading efficiency.** KIM-38 measured 0.48 at 16 threads and 0.16 at 64, on
  128 reduced-model cells at 6,300 s (619 s and 454 s wall). The review's reading
  was GC contention, not BLAS: user CPU is flat, system CPU grows, and a cell
  allocates about 4.4 MiB per handshake. At 0.16, the 736 CPU-h budget costs about
  3,000 core-h.
- **The scheduler.** `_each` (`src/csmc.jl:386`) uses `Threads.@threads`, which
  hands out contiguous chunks and does not stop on the first error. The
  `_task_cause` unwrapping lives in `_each_cause` (`src/predict_forward.jl:199`), so
  only `predict`'s two call sites keep their error types.
- **Simplifications E1 and E2**, a missing end-to-end test for
  `MetabolicBlockStalled`, and helpers for M5's scripts.
- **The freeze's condition** (spec §12, 2026-10-30): any change to `predict` after
  the freeze must give the frozen code's results on the same seeds before any M5
  job. It is shown old against new, and threaded against serial.

The frozen code is main at `93abbce`, the M4 merge. Its last code commit is
`c56f5a6`.

**Your calls (2026-10-30):**

**2. `_each` becomes a greedy scheduler that stops on the first error**
(`src/csmc.jl`).
- **The signature stays `(f, n, threads)`.** `dev/scripts/dose_screen_kim37.jl`
  calls it, and it is not edited.
- **How it runs:**
  - It spawns `min(n, nthreads())` tasks. Each takes the next `i` from a
    `Threads.Atomic{Int}` counter until `i > n` or a stop flag is set.
  - An error inside `f(i)` is recorded under a lock, keeping the lowest `i`, and
    sets the stop flag. After `@sync`, that error is thrown as itself.
  - The counter hands out indices in order, and in-flight items finish. So the
    error thrown is the serial loop's: the lowest failing index.
- **What goes:** `_each_cause` and `_task_cause` are deleted. The five call sites
  (`csmc:598`, `block1:140`, `gibbs:156`, and `predict_forward`'s two) keep their
  error types.
- **Unchanged:** a stalled cell is caught inside `_hybrid_cell` and returned as
  `:not_advanced`. It is a result, not an error, so it never stops a matrix. The
  comment says so.
- **Tests** (`test/test_csmc.jl`):
  - every index runs exactly once, for n = 0, 1, fewer than the threads, and many
    more;
  - the lowest failing index's error is thrown as its own type, threaded and not;
  - at more than one thread, an early failure stops most of the remaining work
    (items sleep 1 ms; fewer than n/2 run).
- **The existing tests** cover the other call sites: the 16a.9a
  threaded-equals-serial tests, and `test_predict.jl:212`'s typed-error test.

**3. The simplifications (E1, E2)**
- **`src/predict_forward.jl`:**
  - `_run_matrix(f, values, seeds, quantity; threads)` loses its `run` mask. An
    entry of `nothing` in `values` is not run.
  - `_run_cells` becomes `only(_run_matrix(f, fill(values, 1, 1), reshape(seeds, :, 1, 1), …))`.
    The mean field still runs one solve.
- **`src/predict.jl`:**
  - **One baseline pool.** When `nv > 0`, μ̂'s cells and V's run in one
    `_run_matrix` call on `cat(seeds, vseeds; dims = 1)`, split back into `cells`
    and `vcells`. Each cell is fixed by its seed, so the results do not change. The
    docstring's "Parallel runs" paragraph then describes the code as it is.
  - The intervened arm passes `nothing` for the pairs V rejects.
  - `_status_counts(cells)` serves both `statuses` and `statuses_intervened`.
    **What they count does not change:** `statuses` still leaves out V's cells.
- **`src/predict_variance.jl`:** `clip(x) = max(x, 0.0)`. `max` already propagates
  NaN, and `-0.0` maps to `0.0` as before.

## Stage 1: the diagnosis (Slurm; no `src` change follows from it before the gate)

`dev/scripts/threading_kim68.jl` and `.sh` are the first users of the helpers.

**The matrix.** KIM-38's: d11, `v6`, `completion = :prior`, M 4 × K 8 × 4 cells =
128 cells at 6,300 s.
- The draws, θ_H and seeds come from the same rng's draws, which do not depend on
  the horizon. A 60-s `predict` with `Xoshiro(18_438_301)` gives them; the script
  asserts this.
- The instrumentation is in the script, wrapped around `InferCell._hybrid_cell`.
  `src` is not touched.

**The stages** (a SMOKE pass first, end to end):

| stage | job | records |
|---|---|---|
| `serial` | 1 core | 8 cells: per-cell `@timed` (time, gctime, bytes) |
| `threaded` | 16 threads, and 64 threads at default GC | first 2 serial cells (node-matched baseline), then all 128 cells under (i) the frozen chunked `@threads` loop and (ii) the new `_each`. Per cell: `@timed`, `threadid()`, start and stop `time_ns`. Totals: `Base.gc_time_ns()`, `Base.gc_num()`, MaxRSS. Also the wall time of one full `predict` call |
| `threaded` variants | 64 threads, the new `_each` only | `--gcthreads=32,1`; `--gcthreads=1`; `OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1` (rules BLAS out) |
| `procs` | one 64-core node as `srun` 4 tasks × 16 threads | 32 cells each: do isolated heaps restore per-cell time, without `Distributed`? |
| `allocs` | 1 core | `Profile.Allocs` on one 60-s cell: the top sites by bytes |

- **Efficiency** = 128 × the node's serial median / (threads × wall).
- **The cost** is about 100 core-h.
- **The note:** `dev/scripts/threading_kim68_result.md`, with the job ids, nodes,
  archive `SHA256SUMS` digest and commit. It gives:
  - the GC share of cell time;
  - per-cell slowdown against serial;
  - the tail;
  - what each variant changes;
  - the allocation sites.
