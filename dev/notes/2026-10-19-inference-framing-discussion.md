## 0. The short version

- We do not try to infer every parameter of a whole-cell model jointly.
- "Modular" can mean two different cuts. A **statistical cut** stops some data
  from updating some parameters. A **dynamical reduction** drops a weak edge in
  the simulator and keeps full Bayes on what is left. The second can keep the
  cross-module information we care about. The first removes it.
- Information travels across the seam on the interface trajectory, never on a
  parameter. Forward, it is a distribution. Backward, it is a likelihood.
- In the reduced syn3A model the seam is two-way in topology and nearly one-way
  in gain, and the measured information follows the gain.
- Under bulk metabolites the stochastic paths may not need to be sampled at all.
  One number, R, says when.
- **Paper 1 becomes one combined paper:** the framework, plus an approximate
  inference method validated against the exact sampler. The exact sampler is the
  reference, not the headline.

## 1. The question

The user's question: how exactly do we do inference, and what is the goal? Joint
inference over ~1000 parameters does not scale. So: (1) modular Bayesian
inference, where modules are fitted with different data; and (2) how uncertainty
and information travel between modules.

A third party answered first, then reviewed our reply, then reviewed it again.
This note records where the three positions converged.

## 2. Three problems that get conflated

| Problem | Statement |
|---|---|
| Local inference | What do a subsystem's own data say about its parameters? |
| Uncertainty propagation | Given uncertain outputs of one module, how uncertain are the predictions downstream? |
| Cross-module inference | Do observations of one module inform the parameters of another, when no parameter is shared? |

The third is the interesting one. It is also what F6's ptsG cell is.

## 3. Three graphs

| Graph | Nodes and edges | In Core A′ |
|---|---|---|
| **Coupling** (dynamical) | Which module drives which state. Each edge has a gain | Two-way: enzymes and drains one way, pools the other |
| **Likelihood dependence** | Which likelihood factors depend on which parameters, after the latent paths are integrated out | A gene-expression parameter appears in the transcript factor *and* the metabolite factor |
| **Information** | Which data streams materially move which parameters | Sparse and directional: metabolites → ptsG strong; transcripts → ENO null |

The three need not have the same sparsity or direction. How they relate may be
InferCell's central mathematical idea.

**Wording, corrected by the third party:** marginalising the paths does not make
a parameter "shared". Each parameter stays local to its rate laws. What changes
is which likelihood factors depend on it. The practical point stands: the
modular note's §7 table rules out EP, copies-and-tie and SMI because no
parameter is shared. That holds only while we condition on the path.

## 4. Two kinds of cut

| | Statistical cut | Dynamical reduction |
|---|---|---|
| What changes | The inference target. The simulator stays | The simulator. The inference stays full Bayes |
| Example | Fit gene expression to transcripts only, then push forward | Rebuild the rate constants from reference pools, not live ones |
| Effect on ptsG under metabolites only | Removed by construction | Kept, because it travels on the strong forward edge |
| Cost | A different target, not a posterior | A labelled reduction whose cost is measurable |

The 10-13 note's Path 1 and the third party's first "Case B" are statistical
cuts. §12 rejected the cut on 2026-09-03 and on 2026-10-05, because it removes
the ptsG cell.

**Treat a dynamical reduction as an inference approximation.** The full,
two-way model stays the scientific reference, and data for every test are
generated by it. Phase 14c already did this: its sampler model had a smoothed
drain and continuous pools, the data came from the published model, and 14c.5
measured the cost.

## 5. How information travels

Write `u` for the interface trajectory (enzyme counts and drains one way, pools
the other). For a one-way pair A → B:

- **Forward (uncertainty):** `p(u | D_A)`.
- **Backward (information):** `λ_B(u) = p(D_B | u) = ∫ p(D_B | θ_B, u) p(θ_B) dθ_B`.
  A likelihood over the interface, never a posterior over parameters.
- **Joint:** `p(u | D_A, D_B) ∝ p(u | D_A) · λ_B(u)`. Given `u`, each module's
  parameters are local.

`∫ p(θ_B | D_B, u) q(u | D_A) du` omits `λ_B`. That is the cut. The whole
difference between "uncertainty propagation" and "information flow" is whether
`λ_B` is multiplied in.

With feedback, `u` is a loop unrolled in time, and the exact answer is
forward–backward over time. The conditional SMC path update already does that.
**So the exact sampler is modular inference with nothing dropped.**

## 6. What the numbers say: information follows gain

| Direction | Gain | Information | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gene expression → pools | Order one (ATP turns over in ~109 s, GTP in ~30 s) | ptsG under bulk metabolites only: posterior/prior SD 0.10 at 10% CV (noise bracket 0.19). Linearised, Core A′, 200 cells | D9; `bulk_identifiability_16a7_result.md` |
| Pools → rebuilt rate constants | Transcription 0.044–0.051 over four pools (GTP alone 0.008–0.012). Translation 0.091 | ENO under transcripts only: 0.999 of prior SD. FBA open (curvature upper 2σ ≈ 0.72) | spec §1, §12 2026-10-05; `eno_path_information_16a7_result.md` |

- **The reverse gain is weak because the published rate laws are saturated.**
  `RNAPOL_KD = 0.1` mM (`src/organisms/coreA/transcription.jl:50`) against ATP
  ≈ 3.65 mM. CTP and UTP are clamped, so only ATP and GTP are live.
- **The spec's "likely reason" for the ENO null is the 60 s rebuild.** We think
  saturation dominates: GTP is the fast pool but has an elasticity of ~0.01; ATP
  carries most of the elasticity and turns over in ~109 s. Not established. The
  test: the ENO path-information script with the reverse edge live, frozen, and
  with a 6 s rebuild, compared on likelihoods (~5 CPU-h per run).
- **Gains are a cheap proxy that has already worked.** D9 predicted the asymmetry
  before any posterior existed.
- **The 60 s rebuild is an exact, tiny interface** (ATP, GTP and the charged
  pool at ~105 instants). But it carries almost nothing. The load-bearing
  interface is the other way: the 1 s drains, the catalytic counts and volume.
- **ENO is identified by the metabolites anyway:** 0.13 at 10% CV (bracket
  0.75).

## 7. Marginalising the stochastic paths

Since 2026-10-05 the metabolites are bulk: the mean over C cells, at assay CV
`σ_b`. With the reverse edge reduced, the likelihood factorises exactly as

```
p(D_tx, D_met | θ) = p(D_tx | θ_G) · p(D_met | D_tx, θ_G, θ_M)
```

- **Transcript factor: closed form.** Each gene is a birth–death chain with a
  piecewise-constant birth rate, observed exactly every 60 s.
  `src/transcript_bridge.jl` already computes the exact transition probabilities.
  Low copy number does not hurt an exact likelihood.
- **Metabolite factor: the hard part.** One cell's path moves `x̄` by about
  1/C, but the metabolites can still constrain the C paths jointly (the third
  party's objection, which is right).

### R: the number that decides the regime

Draw the C paths from `p(X | θ, D_tx)` and score the bulk likelihood on them.
The variance of that log-likelihood is about

```
R ≈ Σ_i Var(log x̄_i | θ, D_tx) / σ_b²  ≈  n · (CV_cell / √C)² / σ_b²
```

Correlations across metabolites and times do not reduce it in expectation. They
enter only at second order, through tr(S²).

| Setting | n | Per-cell CV | R |
|---|---|---|---|
| M0 (C = 50, σ_b = 0.1) | 170 (17 pools × 10 saves) | 1.0 | ~340 |
| M0 | 170 | 0.1 | ~3.4 |
| Core A′ (C = 200) | ~1,800 (17 × ~105) | 0.1 | ~9 |

| Regime | What it means | Method |
|---|---|---|
| R ≪ 1 | The paths are a nuisance in the strong sense. The bulk factor barely depends on the transcripts | One draw of paths per θ is a usable estimator. Needs per-cell CVs of a few percent: unlikely for the full panel |
| 1 ≲ R ≲ n | The metabolites constrain the paths collectively | Gaussian convolution: `log z ≈ N(m, σ_b² I + S)`, with `m`, `S` the conditional moments of `log x̄`. Exact under the Gaussian approximation, at any R. Needs the full covariance `S`, which should be low-rank |
| Non-Gaussian | Pools at the one-particle floor (Pi, 3PG, 2PG) or near a clip | Keep path sampling for those pools, or drop them |

`σ_b` in V7 is drawn from LogNormal(log 0.2, 1), so R spans ~25× across V7's 50
datasets. The same datasets show where any approximation breaks.

### Mean field, and the Jensen gap

The gene-expression network is first order: transcription zeroth, decay,
translation and translocation first. So the mean equations close exactly, as
linear ODEs. Given the rate constants, the expected transcripts, proteins and
drains are exact. The approximation enters at two places only:

- **The metabolism is nonlinear.** Feeding it mean inputs gives the Jensen gap.
  The drain clip at zero adds to it.
- **The rate constants read the pools.** E[k(pools)] ≠ k(E[pools]). Second order,
  because the elasticity is 0.04–0.09.

Feedback: rebuild from the **population-mean pools under the current θ**, not
from fixed nominal pools. That keeps the autocatalytic loop (scoping note,
criterion 4) at population level, and drops only cell-to-cell fluctuations in
the feedback. Measure its cost on the metabolite likelihood too: translation's
reverse channel (0.091) feeds the drains directly, and the ENO script cannot see
that.

## 8. The iterative protocol: a correction

`2026-05-22-calibration-curve.md` blames `iterative_infer`'s overconfidence
mainly on KDE tail truncation. The code says otherwise. Each pass takes the
other block's posterior as its prior and multiplies in its own data again. After
k passes both likelihoods count about k times. Widths should shrink by ~1/√k:
50% at the `max_iters = 4` every seed hit, against 55–60% observed. Moment
matching (that note's follow-up 1) would not fix it. Passing likelihood factors
with a cavity (EP) would. The third party agrees. It does not matter for Core A′
today. It will if parameters become jointly relevant to two factors (§3, §7).

## 9. What an exact posterior on Core A′ costs

The exact sampler, not a naive brute force, at full scale (D16.6 and the 10-13
note; projections, which 16a.11 replaces with a measured sweep):

| | Now | With FBDF + KLU (3.4×, measured, not adopted) |
|---|---|---|
| CPU-h per posterior | ~60k–125k (D16.6 allows up to 150k) | ~18k–37k |
| Wall-clock, 20 particles in parallel | ~1.5 h per sweep, so ~4 months | ~5–6 weeks |
| Wall-clock, serial | ~30 h per sweep, so ~7 years | ~2 years |
| Coverage (R ≥ 50) | × 50: out of reach | × 50: still out of reach |

Assumes 200 cells, 2,000 sweeps, 16.5 to ~35 cycles per cell per sweep. M0 mixes
at 0.11–0.18 effective draws per sweep; Core A′ may mix worse. Cells update in
turn under bulk metabolites, which is why the wall-clock is long.

**One exact Core A′ posterior is expensive, not impossible.** It would give the
approximate method one exact yardstick at full scale. It needs a §12 entry to
adopt the solver (V2's bitwise replay breaks; V4 and V5 rerun) and the cap
reopened.

## 10. Validating an approximate method without an exact reference

Coverage alone is not enough: the prior is perfectly calibrated too. Three
checks:

1. **Calibration.** Truths from the prior, data from the full two-way model,
   coverage and rank-ECDF. A cheap method affords R ≥ 200, sharper than the exact
   sampler's R = 50.
2. **Efficiency.** Posterior widths against the linearised information bound.
   Key cell: ptsG under metabolites only, bound ≈ 0.10 of prior SD at 10% CV. A
   method that is calibrated but stays near the prior has lost the information,
   which is the cut's failure.
3. **Exactness on M0.** Against V6 and V7 directly.

"Modular" does not mean cheap by default. The bulk factor by brute force needs
200 cells per θ, ~1.8 CPU-h per evaluation on Core A′. It is cheap only through
mean field or an emulator.

**Two build routes for the metabolite factor.** (i) A deterministic jump module
with no reactions inside the hybrid driver (the `HeldEnzymeCounts` pattern):
reuses every edge, ~30 s per evaluation on Core A′, no gradients. (ii) Mean-field
gene expression as ODE modules, with the ODE path taught to run the coupling
edges: real framework work, but fast, differentiable, and a true formalism swap.
**Chosen: (ii)** (A6). It also makes 5.4 reachable.
