\section{Methods}
\label{sec:methods}

\subsection{The hybrid driver}
\label{sec:driver}

The driver keeps two blocks with separate state: metabolite pools in mM as floating
point values, and molecule counts as integers. One handshake step does the
following, in order:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item update growth and cell geometry from the membrane protein counts;
  \item write the protein counts into the metabolic rate laws (catalytic edges);
  \item step the ODE block by 1\,s (Rodas5P, absolute tolerance $\pn{fw.abstol}$, relative
  tolerance $\pn{fw.reltol}$);
  \item debit the pools by the costs of the stochastic events of the previous step
  (deferred-cost edges), clipped at zero as in the published model;
  \item every 60\,s, rebuild the stochastic rate constants from the pools;
  \item step the jump block by 1\,s with Gillespie's direct method
  \cite{gillespie1977exact}.
\end{enumerate}
On the jump clock the rebuilt constants therefore take effect at $60m - 1$\,s. Pools
are written back as molecule counts under a declared rounding policy. A recorded path
can be replayed: the replay applies the recorded firings instead of drawing new ones,
and it returns the log density of the path and its sufficient statistics (event
counts and exposures).

\subsection{Observation model}
\label{sec:obs}

Each synthetic dataset has $C$ cells (\pn{mzero.cells} on M0), observed every 60\,s.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Transcripts:} the exact count of each gene's transcripts in each cell
  at each save.
  \item \textbf{Bulk metabolites:} one measurement per pool per save of the mean over
  the $C$ cells, $\bar{x}$, with lognormal noise: $\log z \sim
  \mathcal{N}(\log \bar{x}, \sigb^2)$. The assay noise $\sigb$ is a free parameter.
\end{itemize}
We use bulk metabolites because a per-cell metabolite panel cannot be measured.
The panel has \pn{mzero.npanel} pools: G6P, F6P, intracellular and extracellular
lactate, G3P, DHAP, charged tRNA, phosphate, 3PG, 2PG, FDP, ATP, ADP, AMP, GTP, GDP and
GMP. The prior on the assay noise is $\sigb \sim \mathrm{LogNormal}(\log 0.2,\, 1)$.

\subsection{The exact sampler}
\label{sec:sampler}

The sampler is a three-block Gibbs scan with the stochastic path as a latent variable.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Paths.} Conditional sequential Monte Carlo over 60\,s windows
  \cite{andrieu2010particle}, with particle Gibbs at $N = \pn{mzero.N}$ particles. Each
  particle is a snapshot of the driver. Transcripts are proposed as exact
  endpoint-conditioned birth--death bridges \cite{hobolth2009simulation}, so every
  proposal agrees with the observed transcript counts. The other reactions are then
  simulated given the transcripts. The weight is the bulk metabolite likelihood
  times each gene's transition probability. Cells are updated in turn, because the
  bulk likelihood couples them.
  \item \textbf{Metabolic constants and $\sigb$.} A univariate slice step on each log
  parameter, given the paths. Reverse constants follow from the forward ones and the
  equilibrium constants.
  \item \textbf{Gene-expression constants.} Given the path, each promoter strength
  and the decay rate have an exact one-dimensional conditional of the form
  $n u - e^{u} A + \log p(u)$, where $n$ is the event count and $A$ the exposure. We
  sample it by a slice step.
\end{enumerate}
The sampler is written directly, not in a probabilistic programming language. The
path update needs ancestor sampling and exact snapshots of the driver, and a general
framework could not provide them without sharing solver state between particles.
Runs can be checkpointed and resumed bit for bit, and the threaded sweep is bit for
bit equal to the serial one.

\subsection{The transcript factor}
\label{sec:txfactor}

When the paths are integrated out, each gene's transcripts form a birth--death chain
with a piecewise-constant birth rate, observed exactly every 60\,s. The likelihood of
one cell's record is a product of transition probabilities. We compute them by
uniformisation with tridiagonal products, truncated where the remaining mass is below
$\pn{fw.trunc}$. The cap on the count is fixed per dataset, and the number of terms comes
from the primal value, so the factor is differentiable in the rates.

\subsection{The mean-field route}
\label{sec:meanfield}

The swap replaces the three jump modules with one mean-field module. Its equations
are generated from the jump modules' own propensities and update rules. The composed
model is one ODE problem. Four edges change form, and each change is a declared
reduction:
\begin{itemize}
  \item catalytic edges read the mean protein counts;
  \item drains are continuous and not clipped, and the solve stops if a pool becomes
  negative;
  \item the rebuild is a discrete callback at $60m - 1$\,s and at $t = 0$;
  \item growth is continuous.
\end{itemize}
The bulk factor is $\mathcal{N}\!\left(\log z;\ \log \max(\bar{x}_{\mathrm{MF}}(\theta), 1)
+ \delta,\ \sigb^2 I + S\right)$, with $\delta$ and $S$ from an ensemble of the exact
hybrid at a reference parameter value, and the floor of one molecule. The reference
value is the maximum a posteriori estimate at $\delta = 0$ and $S = 0$, recorded per
dataset. The ensemble has \pn{mix.nref} cells, on seeds disjoint from the dataset's. We
take $\delta$ as the log difference between the ensemble's mean and the mean field at
the reference value, per pool and save. We take $S$ by the delta method, as the
covariance of the per-cell panel counts divided by $\bar{x}\bar{x}^{\mathsf{T}} C$,
elementwise. On M0 it is full rank, with \pn{mzero.npanel} pools at ten saves.

The sampler is the No-U-Turn Sampler on the log of the \pn{mzero.npar} parameters, with
the exact sampler's priors. Each fit has \pn{mix.chains} chains of \pn{mix.nadapt}
adaptation and \pn{mix.ndraw} sampling steps, started \pn{mix.jitter} prior standard
deviations from the reference value. A parameter value outside the transcript factor's
support, or a solve that does not succeed, has a log density of $-\infty$.

\subsection{Simulation-based calibration}
\label{sec:sbc}

For each of $R = \pn{vseven.R}$ replicates we drew a parameter set from the prior,
simulated a dataset, and ran one chain of \pn{mzero.sweeps} sweeps from a prior draw. We dropped
\pn{mzero.burn} sweeps and thinned the rest to $L = \pn{mzero.L}$ draws. The rank of the truth among the
draws is uniform if the sampler is calibrated \cite{talts2018validating}. We tested
each parameter's rank-ECDF against a simultaneous band at $1 - 0.05/6$
\cite{sailynoja2022graphical}, so the test as a whole is at the 95\% level. The pass
rule was fixed before any rank was computed.

\subsection{Prediction}
\label{sec:prediction}

\paragraph{What was fixed in advance.} We fixed the quantity $Q$, the estimand, the
viability rule, the dose and the variance split on pilots with held-out parameters and
seeds, before any evidence run, and recorded them in the project's amendment log. The
pilot's sensitivity rule for $Q$ asked $\theta_I$ to hold at least 5\% of the parameter
variance at \pn{model.cycle}\,s, and lactate export did not meet it
(\pn{pilot.share}\%~$\pm$~\pn{pilot.sharese}\%). We kept $Q$, on a reading written after a
small trial run and before the full pilot: the failure counts against $Q$ only if
$\theta_I$'s elasticities are under a quarter of $\theta_H$'s, and they are not
(\pn{pilot.elI} against \pn{pilot.elH}). The small share comes from the tight posterior.

\paragraph{Estimand.} For a parameter draw, $\hat\mu(\theta)$ is the mean of $Q$ over
$n$ hybrid cells with fresh seeds. Its noise is $\sigma^2(\theta)/n$, and we subtract the
mean of that term from the spread over draws. The intrinsic spread is reported
separately, from the hybrid only.

\paragraph{Completion and viability.} On the reduced model, $\theta_H$ is drawn from its
prior, and width scales its log-scale standard deviation at a fixed median. A cell is
failed, starved, below a floor, clipped or clean, in that order of precedence, and a cell
is starved if some counter clipped at more than half its drains. A parameter pair is
viable if at least half of \pn{budget.ncellspair} separate cells are viable, where clipped
cells count as viable. We condition $\theta_H$ on viability per draw of $\theta_I$, so
$\theta_I$'s marginal stays the M0 posterior, given at least one viable $\theta_H$ for it.
The joint variant gave the same shares to the reported precision.

\paragraph{The variance split.} With a crossed matrix of $\hat\mu$ we use weighted
U-statistics on the centred values, and we report both nested orders. We checked the
estimator's bias by simulation on known splits. The standard errors are a two-way
jackknife, checked on synthetic matrices with the same structure. Near a share of one that
check failed narrowly, so the widest setting's standard error may be too wide by up to
about half.

\paragraph{The intervention.} A knockdown at dose $d$ scales the ptsG promoter strength
by $1 - d$ after the parameter draw, and the effect is paired on common seeds. We assume
that the intervention acts only through that parameter, that the posterior fitted to
unperturbed data carries over unchanged, and that the model stays valid at the dose. $V$
is read at baseline: a rejected pair is not run under the intervention, and a cell that
fails only under it is counted as part of the prediction. The largest dose in
$\{0.1, 0.2, 0.3\}$ that kept M0's pools in their regime was \pn{budget.dose}.

\paragraph{Scoring on M0.} Each posterior contributes \pn{pred.ndraws} draws at
\pn{pred.ncells} cells each. The truth and the offset $\delta$ each use \pn{pred.nref}
cells. Coverage counts the truth's estimated value inside the central interval of the
draws.
