\section{Methods}
\label{sec:methods}

This appendix gives what reproduction needs. \Cref{sec:driver,sec:obs,sec:sampler,sec:txfactor,sec:meanfield,sec:sbc} describe the driver, the data and the two inference routes, as used on M0. \Cref{sec:prediction} covers prediction on M0 and the budget transferred from it. \Cref{sec:reduceddesign} gives the design of the joint posterior and the scored predictions on the reduced model.

\subsection{The hybrid driver}
\label{sec:driver}

The driver keeps two blocks with separate state: metabolite pools in mM as floating
point values, and molecule counts as integers. One handshake step does the
following, in order:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item update growth and cell geometry from the membrane protein counts;
  \item write the protein counts into the metabolic rate laws (catalytic edges);
  \item step the ODE block by 1\,s (Rodas5P, absolute tolerance $\pn{fw.abstol}$, relative
  tolerance $\pn{fw.reltol}$);
  \item debit the pools by the costs of the stochastic events of the previous step
  (deferred-cost edges), clipped at zero as in the published model;
  \item every 60\,s, rebuild the stochastic rate constants from the pools;
  \item step the jump block by 1\,s with Gillespie's direct method
  \cite{gillespie1977exact}.
\end{enumerate}
On the jump clock the rebuilt constants therefore take effect at $60m - 1$\,s. Pools
are written back as molecule counts under a declared rounding policy. A recorded path
can be replayed: the replay applies the recorded firings instead of drawing new ones,
and it returns the log density of the path and its sufficient statistics (event
counts and exposures).

\subsection{Observation model}
\label{sec:obs}

Each synthetic dataset has $C$ cells (\pn{mzero.cells} on M0 and \pn{kim92.ncells} on the reduced model), observed every 60\,s.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Transcripts:} the exact count of each gene's transcripts in each cell
  at each save.
  \item \textbf{Bulk metabolites:} one measurement per pool per save of the mean over
  the $C$ cells, $\bar{x}$, with lognormal noise: $\log z \sim
  \mathcal{N}(\log \bar{x}, \sigb^2)$. The assay noise $\sigb$ is a free parameter.
\end{itemize}
We use bulk metabolites because a per-cell metabolite panel cannot be measured.
The panel has \pn{mzero.npanel} pools: G6P, F6P, intracellular and extracellular
lactate, G3P, DHAP, charged tRNA, phosphate, 3PG, 2PG, FDP, ATP, ADP, AMP, GTP, GDP and
GMP. The prior on the assay noise is $\sigb \sim \mathrm{LogNormal}(\log 0.2,\, 1)$.

\subsection{The exact sampler}
\label{sec:sampler}

The sampler is a three-block Gibbs scan with the stochastic path as a latent variable.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Paths.} Conditional sequential Monte Carlo over 60\,s windows
  \cite{andrieu2010particle}, with particle Gibbs at $N = \pn{mzero.N}$ particles. Each
  particle is a snapshot of the driver. Transcripts are proposed as exact
  endpoint-conditioned birth--death bridges \cite{hobolth2009simulation}, so every
  proposal agrees with the observed transcript counts. The other reactions are then
  simulated given the transcripts. The weight is the bulk metabolite likelihood
  times each gene's transition probability. Cells are updated in turn, because the
  bulk likelihood couples them.
  \item \textbf{Metabolic constants and $\sigb$.} A univariate slice step on each log
  parameter, given the paths. Reverse constants follow from the forward ones and the
  equilibrium constants.
  \item \textbf{Gene-expression constants.} Given the path, each promoter strength
  and the decay rate have an exact one-dimensional conditional of the form
  $n u - e^{u} A + \log p(u)$, where $n$ is the event count and $A$ the exposure. We
  sample it by a slice step.
\end{enumerate}
The sampler is written directly, not in a probabilistic programming language. The
path update needs ancestor sampling and exact snapshots of the driver, and a general
framework could not provide them without sharing solver state between particles.
Runs can be checkpointed and resumed bit for bit, and the threaded sweep is bit for
bit equal to the serial one. We run the exact sampler on M0 only. On the reduced model one
exact posterior is projected at about \pn{cost.fullscale} CPU-hours (\cref{tab:cost}), so
the joint posterior there comes from the mixed route (\cref{sec:reduceddesign}).

\subsection{The transcript factor}
\label{sec:txfactor}

When the paths are integrated out, each gene's transcripts form a birth--death chain
with a piecewise-constant birth rate, observed exactly every 60\,s. The likelihood of
one cell's record is a product of transition probabilities. We compute them by
uniformisation with tridiagonal products, truncated where the remaining mass is below
$\pn{fw.trunc}$. The cap on the count is fixed per dataset, and the number of terms comes
from the primal value, so the factor is differentiable in the rates.

\subsection{The mean-field route}
\label{sec:meanfield}

The swap replaces the three jump modules with one mean-field module. Its equations
are generated from the jump modules' own propensities and update rules. The composed
model is one ODE problem. Four edges change form, and each change is a declared
reduction:
\begin{itemize}
  \item catalytic edges read the mean protein counts;
  \item drains are continuous and not clipped, and the solve stops if a pool becomes
  negative;
  \item the rebuild is a discrete callback at $60m - 1$\,s and at $t = 0$;
  \item growth is continuous.
\end{itemize}
The bulk factor is
\begin{equation*}
\mathcal{N}\!\left(\log z;\ \log \max(\bar{x}_{\mathrm{MF}}(\theta), 1) + \delta,\ \sigb^2 I + S\right),
\end{equation*}
with $\bar{x}$ floored at one molecule. The offset
$\delta$ and the covariance $S$ come from an ensemble of the exact hybrid at a reference
parameter value. The offset $\delta$ plays the part of a model-discrepancy term \cite{kennedy2001bayesian}, fixed at one reference value. The route multiplies the bulk factor by the transcript factor, so it ignores that both read the same cells.

The reference
value is the maximum a posteriori estimate at $\delta = 0$ and $S = 0$, recorded per
dataset. The ensemble has \pn{mix.nref} cells, on seeds disjoint from the dataset's. We
take $\delta$ as the log difference between the ensemble's mean and the mean field at
the reference value, per pool and save. We take $S$ by the delta method, $S_{ij} = \mathrm{Cov}_{ij} / (C \bar{x}_i \bar{x}_j)$, where $\mathrm{Cov}$ is the covariance of the per-cell panel counts. On M0, $S$ is full rank, with \pn{mzero.npanel} pools at ten saves.

On M0, the sampler is the No-U-Turn Sampler on the log of the \pn{mzero.npar} parameters, with
the exact sampler's priors. Each M0 fit has \pn{mix.chains} chains of \pn{mix.nadapt}
adaptation and \pn{mix.ndraw} sampling steps, started \pn{mix.jitter} prior standard
deviations from the reference value. A parameter value outside the transcript factor's
support, or a solve that does not succeed, has a log density of $-\infty$.
\Cref{sec:reduceddesign} gives the sampler's design on the reduced model.

\subsection{Simulation-based calibration}
\label{sec:sbc}

Calibration runs on M0 only. For each of $R = \pn{vseven.R}$ replicates we drew a parameter set from the prior,
simulated a dataset, and ran one chain of \pn{mzero.sweeps} sweeps from a prior draw. We dropped
\pn{mzero.burn} sweeps and thinned the rest to $L = \pn{mzero.L}$ draws. For near-independent draws, the rank of the truth among them is uniform if the sampler is calibrated \cite{talts2018validating}. We tested
each parameter's rank-ECDF against a simultaneous band at $1 - 0.05/6$
\cite{sailynoja2022graphical}, so the test as a whole is at the 95\% level. The pass
rule was fixed before any rank was computed.

\subsection{Prediction}
\label{sec:prediction}

This subsection covers M0's scored predictions (\cref{sec:predm0}) and the budget transferred
from M0 to the reduced model (\cref{sec:budget}). The scored predictions on the reduced
model use the design of \cref{sec:reduceddesign}.

\paragraph{What was fixed in advance.} We fixed the quantity $Q$, the estimand, the
viability rule, the dose and the variance split on pilots with held-out parameters and
seeds, before any evidence run, and recorded them in the project's amendment log. The
pilot's sensitivity rule for $Q$ asked $\theta_I$ to hold at least 5\% of the parameter
variance at \pn{model.cycle}\,s, and lactate export did not meet it
(\pn{pilot.share}\%~$\pm$~\pn{pilot.sharese}\%). We kept $Q$ under a reading written after a
small trial run and before the full pilot. Under that reading the failure counts against $Q$ only if
$\theta_I$'s elasticities are under a quarter of $\theta_H$'s, and they are not
(\pn{pilot.elI} against \pn{pilot.elH}). The reading suggests that the small share comes from the tight posterior.

\paragraph{Estimand.} For a parameter draw, $\hat\mu(\theta)$ is the mean of one cell's
exported lactate over $n$ hybrid cells with fresh seeds. It estimates the population mean
$\mu(\theta)$, which is $Q$ (\cref{sec:predictapi}) at that draw, and $\mathrm{Var}(\mu)$ is
the parameter variance $\mathrm{Var}_\theta(Q)$ of \cref{sec:reducedpredict}. The noise of $\hat\mu$ is $\sigma^2(\theta)/n$, and we subtract the
mean of that term from the spread over draws. The intrinsic spread is reported
separately, from the hybrid only.

\paragraph{Completion and viability.} In the budget transferred from M0, $\theta_H$ is drawn from its
prior, and width scales its log-scale standard deviation at a fixed median. A cell is
failed, starved, below a floor, clipped or clean, in that order of precedence. A cell
is starved if some counter clipped at more than half its drains. A cell is viable unless it failed, starved or fell below a floor. We set no floors, so clipped cells count. A parameter pair is viable if at least half of \pn{budget.ncellspair} separate cells are. We condition $\theta_H$ on viability per draw of $\theta_I$, so
$\theta_I$'s marginal stays the M0 posterior, given at least one viable $\theta_H$ for it.
A joint variant, which also reweights $\theta_I$, gave the same shares to the reported precision at the one width where the rule acted.

\paragraph{The variance split.} The held share with $\theta_I$ first is \begin{equation} \frac{\mathrm{E}_{\theta_I}\!\left[\mathrm{Var}(\mu \mid \theta_I)\right]}{\mathrm{Var}(\mu)} , \end{equation} and with $\theta_H$ first it is $\mathrm{Var}_{\theta_H}(\mathrm{E}[\mu \mid \theta_H]) / \mathrm{Var}(\mu)$ \cite{saltelli2010variance}. The gap between them is the interaction plus any dependence the rule induces. With a crossed matrix of $\hat\mu$ we use weighted U-statistics on the centred values, and we report both orders. We checked the
estimator's bias by simulation on known splits. The standard errors are a two-way
jackknife, checked on synthetic matrices with the same structure. Near a share of one that
check failed narrowly, so the widest setting's standard error may be too wide by up to
about half.

\paragraph{The intervention.} A knockdown at dose $d$ scales the ptsG promoter strength
by $1 - d$ after the parameter draw, and the effect is paired on common seeds. We make three
assumptions. The intervention acts only through that parameter. The posterior fitted to
unperturbed data carries over unchanged. The model stays valid at the dose; the screen below supports this on M0, and it is in question on the reduced model (\cref{sec:modelform}). The viability rule $V$
is read at baseline. A rejected pair is not run under the intervention, and a cell that
fails only under it is counted as part of the prediction. The largest dose in
$\{0.1, 0.2, 0.3\}$ that kept M0's pools in their regime was \pn{budget.dose}, and the
scored predictions on the reduced model use the same dose (\cref{sec:reduceddesign}).

\paragraph{Scoring on M0.} Each posterior contributes \pn{pred.ndraws} draws at
\pn{pred.ncells} cells each. The offset $\delta_Q$ is a difference in $Q$ itself, not the log offset of \cref{sec:meanfield}. The truth and $\delta_Q$ each use \pn{pred.nref}
cells. Coverage counts the truth's estimated value inside the central interval of the
draws.

\subsection{The reduced-model design}
\label{sec:reduceddesign}

\paragraph{Free parameters and priors.} The joint posterior of \cref{sec:reduced} has
\pn{kim92.ncoord} coordinates: the logs of the \pn{kim92.npromoter} promoter strengths, of
the transcript decay rate, of the forward catalytic constants of ENO and FBA, and of
$\sigb$. Every other constant stays at its default value. Each of the first 20 has a
lognormal prior whose median is the model's nominal value. Its log-scale standard deviation
is \pn{kim92.sdprom} (a factor of two) for the promoters and the decay rate, and \pn{kim92.sdeno}
and \pn{kim92.sdfba} for ENO and FBA. The prior on $\sigb$ is that of \cref{sec:obs}.

\paragraph{The data.} There is one dataset of \pn{kim92.ncells} cells from the true simulator at
$\theta^\ast$, on a seed fixed before its truth was read. The cells run for one cycle of
6,300\,s, and the fit sees only the first half of the record: the \pn{kim92.nsaves} saves at
60--\pn{kim92.fitend}\,s. The later horizon of the scored predictions starts after the window, at
\pn{kim95.horizonlo}\,s, so the fit never sees it.
The observation design is generous, a best case for what such data can reach. Every cell gives the exact transcript count
of all 17 genes at every save, and the bulk panel of \cref{sec:obs} gives
\pn{kim92.ppcrows} rows, 17 pools at each save.

\paragraph{The mixed route on the reduced model.} The bulk factor and the transcript
factors are those of \cref{sec:meanfield,sec:txfactor}, with the reduced model's
mean-field module. The log density is supported on a box of $\pm\pn{kim92.box}$ prior standard
deviations about the prior median. The reference value $\theta_{\mathrm{ref}}$ is the maximum
a posteriori estimate in that box at $\delta = 0$ and $S = 0$. We take $\delta$ and $S$ from a reference ensemble of
\pn{kim92.nref} cells at $\theta_{\mathrm{ref}}$, run to the last fitted save, and hold both
fixed while sampling.

\paragraph{The sampler.} We use the No-U-Turn Sampler \cite{hoffman2014nouturn} in Turing
\cite{ge2018turing}, at a target acceptance of \pn{kim92.accept} and a maximum tree depth of
\pn{kim92.maxdepth}. There are \pn{kim92.nchains} chains, each of \pn{kim92.nadapt} adaptation steps and
\pn{kim92.firstdraws} kept draws, started \pn{kim92.jitter} prior standard deviations from
$\theta_{\mathrm{ref}}$. Under a unit metric the target is badly conditioned, and a probe
chain projected \pn{kim92.probedays} days per chain. Each chain therefore starts from a
warm diagonal metric, the diagonal of $(-H)^{-1}$ at $\theta_{\mathrm{ref}}$. Here $H$ is
the Hessian of the log density, from central differences of the gradient. Scaled by that
metric, the condition number of $-H$ falls from \pn{kim92.kappaunit} to
\pn{kim92.kappawarm}. Adaptation then re-estimates the metric in windows. We chose the warm
metric before any chain started, and it changed no criterion below. Each chain writes a
checkpoint at fixed intervals and resumes from the last one, so a job that reaches its time
limit can continue. One leapfrog step costs about \pn{kim92.grad}\,s.

\paragraph{The kill criteria.} We froze these in a pre-registration before the dataset was
drawn. The fit fails if either of two conditions holds.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Convergence.} Some coordinate has $\Rhat > \pn{kim92.rhatgate}$ or a bulk
  effective sample size below \pn{kim92.essgate}, over the kept draws of all chains, after
  at most one extension. We check on the first \pn{kim92.firstdraws} kept draws per chain. If that
  check fails, each chain continues for \pn{kim92.firstdraws} more draws from its final state, with
  its adapted step size and metric and no new adaptation. We then check once more on all
  the kept draws.
  \item \textbf{Posterior predictive check.} More than \pn{kim92.ppclimit} of the
  \pn{kim92.ppcrows} bulk rows, 5\%, lie outside their central 99\% posterior predictive
  band. The predictive is the fit's own bulk model,
  $\log z_{\mathrm{rep}} \sim \mathcal{N}\!\left(\log \max(\bar{x}_{\mathrm{MF}}(\theta), 1) + \delta,\ \sigb^2 I + S\right)$.
  We evaluate it at \pn{kim92.ppcdraws} posterior draws spaced evenly through the pooled kept draws, with
  \pn{kim92.ppcreps} replicates each. This check tests whether the fitted likelihood reproduces its
  own data. It does not test the mean-field swap, which the scored predictions test
  against the true simulator.
\end{enumerate}
Cost had no cap, and we measured it (\cref{tab:reducedcost}). The first convergence check
failed and the one extension ran (\cref{sec:reduced}), so the posterior is
\pn{kim92.ndraws} pooled draws, $\pn{kim92.nchains} \times (200 + 200)$.

We also report two quantities that no gate uses: whether the truth lies inside each central 90\% interval,
and the contraction of each coordinate $j$,
\begin{equation}
  c_j = 1 - \frac{\mathrm{Var}_{\mathrm{post}}(\theta_j)}{\mathrm{Var}_{\mathrm{prior}}(\theta_j)} .
\end{equation}
The data reach a coordinate when $c_j \ge \pn{kim92.reachgate}$. There is one truth, so the count of
intervals that hold it is not a coverage rate.

\paragraph{The scored predictions.} We push the pooled draws forward with \texttt{predict}
over the full cycle, at baseline and under the ptsG knockdown at dose \pn{kim95.dose}
(\cref{sec:prediction}), on common seeds. We score them against the true simulator at
$\theta^\ast$ on fresh seeds, with the prior predictive beside them. The targets were frozen
with the kill criteria: $Q$, $Q_{\mathrm{kd}}$ and $\Delta Q$, and the 17 pools and 17
transcripts at the \pn{kim95.nsaves} saves of the later horizon. Each target is a
population mean over a draw's cells, and no viability rule is applied on either arm. For
each target we report whether the truth lies inside the central 50\% and 90\% predictive
intervals. We also report the continuous ranked probability score (CRPS) [CITE NEEDED: CRPS, Gneiting and
Raftery 2007] and its CRPS skill against the prior predictive,
$1 - \mathrm{CRPS}_{\mathrm{post}} / \mathrm{CRPS}_{\mathrm{prior}}$. With one truth these
are scores, not coverage rates.

We sized the runs by M0's two rules: cell noise is at most 10\% of the predictive
variance, and the truth's standard error is at most 10\% of the predictive standard
deviation. For one cell's variance $\sigma^2$ and the parameter variance
$\mathrm{Var}_\theta$ of the population mean, they give
\begin{equation}
  n_{\mathrm{cells}} = \left\lceil \frac{9\,\sigma^2}{\mathrm{Var}_\theta} \right\rceil ,
  \qquad
  n_{\mathrm{truth}} = \left\lceil \frac{100\,\sigma^2}{\mathrm{Var}_\theta} \right\rceil .
\end{equation}
Two sizing passes, made before any truth was read, resolved the posterior's
$\mathrm{Var}_\theta$ only for $Q_{\mathrm{kd}}$, so we sized on it. Each arm has
\pn{kim95.ndraws} draws, with \pn{kim95.cells} cells per posterior draw and
\pn{kim95.priorcells} per prior draw, and the truth has \pn{kim95.ntruth} cells per
knockdown arm.
