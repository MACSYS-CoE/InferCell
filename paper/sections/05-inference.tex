\section{Inference on the reduced model}
\label{sec:reduced}

Can the mixed route give one joint posterior over all the free parameters of the
reduced model, from single-cell and bulk data? On one synthetic dataset it can. The
data reach all \pn{kim92.ncoord} parameters, and the truth sits inside \pn{kim92.truthin}
of the \pn{kim92.ncoord} central 90\% intervals. The posterior reproduces its own data,
and it costs about \pn{kim92.cpuh} CPU-hours (\cref{fig:reducedpost,tab:reducedcost}).

\paragraph{The fit.} The fit uses the parameters and dataset of \cref{sec:model}. The free
parameters $\theta$ are the \pn{kim92.npromoter} promoter strengths, the decay rate, ENO, FBA
and $\sigb$, each on the log scale. The \pn{kim92.ncells} cells come from the true simulator
at $\theta^\ast$. Over the \pn{kim92.nsaves} saves up to \pn{kim92.fitend}\,s, the bulk factor
has \pn{kim92.ppcrows} rows, 17 pools at each save, and the rest of the cycle, the later horizon, is held out for
\cref{sec:reducedpredict}. The route is the one defined in \cref{sec:api}, with $\delta$
and $S$ computed once from a reference ensemble of the exact hybrid
(\cref{sec:meanfield}). We ran \pn{kim92.nchains} chains of the No-U-Turn Sampler, with
pass criteria fixed before the dataset was drawn. \Cref{sec:methods} gives the settings
(\cref{sec:reduceddesign}).

\paragraph{Convergence.} The pass criterion is a rank-normalised $\Rhat$ of at most
\pn{kim92.rhatgate} and a bulk effective sample size of at least \pn{kim92.essgate} on
every parameter, after at most one extension of the chains. After the first
\pn{kim92.firstdraws} kept draws per chain, the check failed on two of the
\pn{kim92.ncoord} parameters, a promoter strength and ENO, with a worst $\Rhat$ of
\pn{kim92.rhatfirst}. No chain was stuck. On both parameters the four chain means agreed
to within about a tenth of a posterior standard deviation (SD). The per-chain SDs
differed by up to a quarter, and at this length $\Rhat$ is sensitive to that difference.
After the one extension, to \pn{kim92.extdraws} kept draws per chain, the worst $\Rhat$
is \pn{kim92.rhat} and the smallest bulk effective sample size is \pn{kim92.essmin}.
All results below pool these \pn{kim92.ndraws} draws.

\paragraph{What the data reach.} We measure how far the data move each parameter by its
contraction,
\[
  c = 1 - \mathrm{Var}_{\mathrm{post}} / \mathrm{Var}_{\mathrm{prior}} ,
\]
and say the data reach a parameter when $c \ge \pn{kim92.reachgate}$. Every parameter
has $c \ge \pn{kim92.contrmin}$ (\cref{fig:reducedpost}A). The two lowest are ENO and FBA,
which also have the tightest priors. No marginal stays at its prior. The only strong
posterior correlation is \pn{kim92.corr}, between the GAPD promoter and the decay rate.
The truth $\theta^\ast$ falls inside the central 90\% interval for \pn{kim92.truthin} of
the \pn{kim92.ncoord} parameters (\cref{fig:reducedpost}B). The one outside is a promoter
strength, at \pn{kim92.outz} posterior SD. There is one truth, so this count is not a
coverage rate.

\paragraph{The promoters sit low.} Of the \pn{kim92.npromoter} promoter strengths,
\pn{kim92.promlow} have posterior means below the truth (\cref{fig:reducedpost}B). Their
mean standardised error, (posterior mean $-$ truth)/posterior SD, is \pn{kim92.promz}. If
the errors were calibrated, their mean would be zero to within its standard error. Allowing
for the posterior's correlations among the promoters, the observed mean is about
\pn{kim92.promzse} standard errors from zero. On the rate scale the shift is small: the
promoters sit about \pn{kim92.promshift}\% low on average. The chains start from a reference
value $\theta_{\mathrm{ref}}$, the maximum a posteriori (MAP) estimate with $\delta = S = 0$
(\cref{sec:reduceddesign}), and it has a shift of the same size, with a mean standardised error
of \pn{kim92.mapz}. So the shift is in the likelihood on this dataset, with or without
$\delta$ and $S$, and not in the sampler. The posterior correlates the promoters by only
\pn{kim92.promcorr} on average, so noise that the likelihood represents cannot produce a
common shift this large; the standard error above already allows for it. A shared component
that the likelihood does not represent would not show in these correlations. Two candidates
fit: a fluctuation of this dataset in the pools that every gene's transcription reads, and
a bias from the mean-field approximation. One dataset cannot separate them. We read the
scored predictions of \cref{sec:reducedpredict} with this shift in mind.

\paragraph{The posterior reproduces its own data.} For each of the \pn{kim92.ppcrows}
bulk rows, we drew replicate data from the route's own bulk model at posterior draws and
asked whether the observed value lies in the central 99\% band. \pn{kim92.ppcout} rows
lie outside, against a limit of \pn{kim92.ppclimit} fixed in advance and about
\pn{kim92.ppcexpect} expected by chance (\cref{fig:reducedpost}C). The rows outside
spread across the record. This check tests whether the fitted likelihood reproduces the
data. It does not test the mean-field approximation against the exact hybrid. On the
reduced model, the mixed route also has no calibration test of the kind
\cref{sec:posteriors} ran on M0. The scored predictions of \cref{sec:reducedpredict},
against the true simulator, are the test of the approximation.

\paragraph{The posterior is close to Gaussian.} For each parameter, the Laplace SD, from the
curvature of the fitted log density at $\theta_{\mathrm{ref}}$, is
\pn{kim92.laplo}--\pn{kim92.laphi} times the posterior SD. The posterior means of ENO and
FBA lie \pn{kim92.mapenoz} and \pn{kim92.mapfbaz} posterior SD from $\theta_{\mathrm{ref}}$.
Their marginals are nearly symmetric, with skewness \pn{kim92.skeweno} and
\pn{kim92.skewfba}, so the offset is not skew. $\theta_{\mathrm{ref}}$ is the MAP with
$\delta = S = 0$, not the MAP of the fitted likelihood, so it need not sit at the posterior's
centre. So on this dataset a Laplace approximation would have given the right widths. We know
that only because NUTS ran: the comparison needs the sampled posterior, and on a dataset
whose posterior is far from Gaussian a Laplace fit alone would give no warning.

\paragraph{What it costs.} The whole run costs about \pn{kim92.cpuh} CPU-hours, of which
the chains are about \pn{kim92.chainsall} (\cref{tab:reducedcost}), below our bound of \pn{cost.kone} CPU-hours per posterior. That
is far below the \pn{cost.fullscale} projected for the exact sampler (\cref{tab:cost}).
The limit is wall-clock time, because each chain is one serial job. Each chain took
\pn{kim92.chainwalllo}--\pn{kim92.chainwallhi}\,h for its \pn{kim92.nadapt} adaptation
and \pn{kim92.firstdraws} kept iterations, and \pn{kim92.extwalllo}--\pn{kim92.extwallhi}\,h
more for the extension. One leapfrog step, one gradient of the log density, costs
\pn{kim92.grad}\,s, and each iteration takes about \pn{kim92.steps} steps. A warm diagonal
metric made the run feasible. We took this metric from the Hessian of the fitted log
density at $\theta_{\mathrm{ref}}$, by finite differences of the gradient. Under a unit metric the Hessian's condition number is
\pn{kim92.kappaunit}; under the warm metric it is \pn{kim92.kappawarm}. A probe chain
under the unit metric projected to \pn{kim92.probedays} days per chain.

\paragraph{The modular arm.} The modular arm asks whether a two-stage fit lands close to
the joint posterior. It has two variants. The factor cut fits the transcript factor over
all \pn{kim92.ncoord} parameters in stage one. It then fits ENO, FBA and $\sigb$ to the
bulk factor over the stage-one draws. The data plug-in takes the transcription rates in
stage one from the observed ATP and GTP panel, with no ODE solve. We call a variant close
