\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

All results use synthetic data, and the priors are asserted. We first show what the
framework predicts on the reduced \syn{} model, where no truth can be checked
(\cref{sec:budget}). We then ask whether such a prediction can be trusted, and score
the same pipeline on M0, where a truth is known (\cref{sec:predm0}). The sections after
that report the two inference routes that the prediction rests on
(\cref{sec:exact,sec:jensen,sec:approx}) and their cost (\cref{sec:cost}). Results
marked \emph{unscored} are not compared with any truth. For the scored results, the
test is calibration: over many datasets, a 90\% credible interval must contain the
truth about 90\% of the time \cite{talts2018validating}.

\subsection{A prediction on the reduced model and its uncertainty budget (unscored)}
\label{sec:budget}

\paragraph{What we predict.} The quantity is the lactate a cell exports over one
published cycle of \pn{model.cycle}\,s, in particles, and the estimand is its population
mean $\mu(\theta)$. We fixed the quantity, the estimand, the viability rule and the
intervention on held-out pilots before running any of this evidence
(\cref{sec:prediction}).

\paragraph{The parameters are a modular transfer.} The \pn{budget.nI} inferred
parameters $\theta_I$ come from one exact posterior on M0 (\cref{sec:exact}). The
\pn{budget.nH} promoter strengths $\theta_H$ whose genes M0 cuts get no data, only their
priors and a viability rule $V$. The distribution is a cut,
\begin{equation}
  p_{\mathrm{cut}}(\theta_I, \theta_H \mid D, V)
  = p(\theta_I \mid D, \mathrm{M0})\; p(\theta_H \mid \theta_I, V),
\end{equation}
and not a joint posterior under the reduced model. We use it because an exact posterior
on the reduced model is out of reach (\cref{sec:cost}). The data do not update
$\theta_H$, and $\theta_H$ does not feed back into $\theta_I$. M0 fits $\theta_I$ with
the counterparts of $\theta_H$ held fixed, so this transfer assumes that the posterior
of $\theta_I$ would not move if they varied. We have not tested that assumption.

\paragraph{The budget.} We ran \pn{budget.nrows} draws of $\theta_I$ crossed with
\pn{budget.ncols} draws of $\theta_H$, at each of three prior widths
(\cref{tab:budget,fig:budget}). At the default width, among the \pn{budget.ndrawn}
parameters drawn and on this one posterior's $\theta_I$, about
\pn{budget.heldpct}\%~$\pm$~\pn{budget.heldpctse}\% of the parameter uncertainty in
lactate export comes from the \pn{budget.nH} promoter strengths that M0's fit cannot
reach. Their medians are a proteomics proxy, and their spread (geometric standard
deviation \pn{budget.gsdone}) is our choice. The share runs from
\pn{budget.heldpctlo}\% at half that spread to \pn{budget.heldpcthi}\% at double. The
two nested orders of the split differ by about a point or less. Every other constant is
held fixed or derived, so this is a split among these \pn{budget.ndrawn} parameters
only, and it is not a property of the model alone.

The share is of the variance of $\mu$. A new cell's variance also holds the cell's own
spread, and of that total the held parameters' part at the default width is
\pn{budget.heldnewcell}\%. The prediction itself moves with the prior too: mean export
rises by a factor of \pn{budget.meanratio}~$\pm$~\pn{budget.meanratiose} from the
narrowest width to the widest.

\paragraph{The knockdown.} We scaled the ptsG promoter strength by $1 - d$, at dose
$d = \pn{budget.dose}$, with paired seeds. At the default width, mean lactate export falls
by \pn{budget.dqpct}\%~$\pm$~\pn{budget.dqpctse}\%. This effect shrinks as the prior
widens, and at the widest setting its interval of two standard errors reaches zero. At
\pn{budget.ncellspair} cells per pair, the run cannot resolve the parameter variance of
$\Delta Q$, because cell noise swamps it. So we report its mean and no variance split
for it.

\paragraph{Viability and validity.} The viability rule rejected nothing at the two
narrower widths, so there the conditioning removed nothing. At the widest it removed
\pn{budget.rejected}\% of pairs, all from three of the \pn{budget.ncols} draws of
$\theta_H$. Clipping is the common state in this run, and the rule allows it: most cells
clip a pool at least once (\cref{tab:budget}). A likely cause is this posterior's low
transcript decay rate. Across the decay rate's deciles at the default width, the clipped
fraction falls from \pn{budget.clipdechi}\% to \pn{budget.clipdeclo}\%. We have not shown
this link on stochastic cells. Nearly every cell's GTP falls at least once below the
half-saturation constant of RNA polymerase, the regime where the basis of the one-way
nucleotide reduction is in question. The run stores no elasticities, so it shows the pool
reaching that regime and not the basis failing. These figures, and the parameter variance,
hold for this one posterior, and another could give different ones.

\paragraph{What this does not show.} It is not calibration, on the reduced model or
anywhere else, and it does not compare the reduced model with real measurements. The
posterior of $\theta_I$ is a fit to M0's synthetic data over \pn{mzero.horizon}\,s, so
its small share is not what real measurements would leave.

\subsection{Scoring the same pipeline on M0}
\label{sec:predm0}

\paragraph{Why M0, and why scoring stops there.} M0 is the largest slice of the reduced
model where a truth can be checked. It has \pn{mzero.ngenes} of the \pn{model.genes}
genes stochastic and inferred, with the other \pn{budget.nH} proteins at their
proteomics counts, over \pn{mzero.horizon}\,s, and the same reactions, blocks and code.
One exact posterior on the reduced model is projected at about \pn{cost.fullscale}
CPU-hours, and calibration needs \pn{mzero.cells} of them, about \pn{cost.fullcoverage}
CPU-hours.

\paragraph{The design.} For each of \pn{vseven.R} datasets with known truth, we pushed
two posteriors through three forward models. The posteriors are the exact sampler's
(\cref{sec:exact}) and the mixed route's (\cref{sec:approx}). The forward models are the
hybrid, the mean field, and the mean field with an offset $\delta$ for this quantity,
taken from a new hybrid ensemble. On M0 nothing is completed from a prior, so there is
no viability filter. We fixed the scoring before the run. With \pn{pred.ndraws} draws, a
calibrated arm's intervals cover \pn{pred.ref90}\% at nominal 90\% and about
\pn{pred.ref50}\% at nominal 50\%, with standard errors over \pn{vseven.R} datasets of
about \pn{pred.se90} and \pn{pred.se50} points. Those are the references we compare
with.

\paragraph{Lactate export.} The exact posterior with the hybrid, and the mixed posterior
with the hybrid, cover at the reference (\cref{tab:crossed,fig:crossed}). The exact arm
is high at nominal 50\%, by \pn{pred.q.eh.sehigh} standard errors. The mixed route's
intervals are about \pn{pred.q.mixnarrow}\% narrower. Changing the forward description
shifts the prediction by \pn{pred.q.fshift} standard deviations at the same width, and
$\delta$ removes that shift. Changing the inference route shifts it by
\pn{pred.q.rshift}. The cheapest route, the mixed posterior with the mean field, covers
\pn{pred.q.cheaplo}--\pn{pred.q.cheaphi}\% at nominal 90\%, about one standard error low.
Its small shift without $\delta$ is the two effects nearly cancelling, not the absence of
either. \pn{vseven.R} datasets cannot separate these arms at 90\%: all six are within
\pn{pred.q.maxse} standard errors of the reference.

\paragraph{The knockdown effect.} A model fitted to unperturbed data predicts the effect
of the knockdown with the right sign and scale. The true effect has a median of
\pn{pred.dqmedian} particles, and every arm's 90\% interval excludes zero with the right
sign on all \pn{vseven.R} datasets. We read this off after the run. These coverages are
not a clean calibration. We sized the run on $Q$, with the rules that cell noise be at
most 10\% of the spread and the truth's standard error at most 10\% of the predictive
standard deviation. The panel fails both rules for $\Delta Q$ on all \pn{vseven.R}
datasets, because pairing removes little of the cells' spread: the noise share has a
median of \pn{pred.dqmcv} under the exact posterior. So the hybrid arms are too wide
because of noise, not because the posterior is. The mean-field arms carry no such term,
so the truth's error counts against them. The mixed posterior with the mean field is the
one arm clearly low: \pn{pred.d.mm.c}\% and \pn{pred.d.md.c}\% at nominal 50\%, and
\pn{pred.d.md.n}\% at nominal 90\% with $\delta$. Noise explains part of that, and the
rest cannot be separated from that route's narrower intervals at these sizes. Meeting the
rules would cost on the order of \pn{pred.dqcost} CPU-hours, and we did not run it.

\paragraph{What this supports.} On M0, the pipeline behind the reduced model's prediction
covers at the expected rate on $Q$, and it gets the sign and scale of the knockdown
right. This panel is the only validation behind the reduced model's knockdown
prediction. It does not test the transfer from M0 into the reduced model.

\subsection{The exact sampler is calibrated on M0}
\label{sec:exact}

\paragraph{Convergence.} We ran \pn{vsix.chains} chains of \pn{vsix.sweeps} sweeps on
one M0 dataset of \pn{mzero.cells} cells. The rank-normalised $\Rhat$
\cite{vehtari2021rank} is at most \pn{vsix.rhat}, and the bulk effective sample
size is at least \pn{vsix.ess} for every parameter. This reference run cost
\pn{vsix.cpuh} CPU-hours.

\paragraph{Calibration.} We drew \pn{vseven.R} parameter sets from the prior,
simulated one dataset from each, and fitted each dataset with one chain. For each
parameter we compared the empirical CDF of the ranks of the truth with a
simultaneous band \cite{sailynoja2022graphical}, at a joint level of 95\% over the
\pn{mzero.npar} parameters. All \pn{mzero.npar} parameters stay inside their bands
(\cref{fig:calibration}a). The largest deviation is \pn{vseven.maxdev}, for the
transcript decay rate, where the band allows \pn{vseven.band}. We also read coverage off the same ranks, for central credible intervals at five
levels from 50\% to 99\%. In \pn{vseven.cov} parameter--level pairs the coverage is
inside the binomial 95\% band. One or two misses over 30 pairs are expected by chance.
The one miss is the decay rate at 99\%, where $L = \pn{mzero.L}$ draws per chain give
the coarsest ranks.

\paragraph{A production-length chain matches the reference.} One chain of the
length we used in the calibration runs is not distinguishable from the reference on
any marginal (\cref{fig:calibration}b). The means differ by at most
\pn{ften.dmean} reference standard deviations, and the ratio of standard deviations
is between \pn{ften.sdlo} and \pn{ften.sdhi}. This chain cost \pn{ften.cpuh}
CPU-hours, about \pn{ften.speedup} times less than the reference.

\paragraph{Limits.} The calibration result holds for M0 only. In \pn{vseven.lowess}
of the \pn{vseven.R} replicates the smallest bulk effective sample size is below 100.
The production chain separates the FBA constant from its matched null by a small
margin, \pn{ften.fbamargin} standard deviations.

\subsection{The cost of the formalism swap is declared and measured}
\label{sec:jensen}

The mean-field swap replaces the stochastic gene expression with its mean. The
means of transcripts and proteins are exact, but metabolism is nonlinear. A
metabolism fed the mean drain differs from the mean over cells of metabolisms fed
their own drains. The difference is a Jensen gap.

\paragraph{The cause.} The gap is largest for GTP, where the mean field sits
\pn{jg.gtplo}--\pn{jg.gtphi}\% below the population mean. We traced it to
cell-to-cell variation in the drains. When every cell receives the mean field's
drains, the mean over cells equals the mean field to three significant figures. We
ruled out the enzyme counts, the per-cell feedback and the clip at zero as causes of
the GTP gap. A likely mechanism, which we have not tested, is that only saturable
enzymes regenerate GTP. Then cells that spend more GTP lose more of it than cells that
spend less gain back, and the population mean sits above GTP at the mean spend.

\paragraph{The correction.} We treat the gap as a declared reduction and correct it
with an offset $\delta$, the log difference between the mean of a reference
ensemble and the mean field at one reference parameter set. Our reference is the
most detailed composition we have, the exact hybrid, and not the truth about the
cell. We report the gap with a goodness check and with the parameter bias it
implies, and we do not use it as a stop.
\begin{itemize}
  \item Without $\delta$, the gap would move the transcript decay rate by
  \pn{jg.biaslo}--\pn{jg.biashi} posterior standard deviations.
  \item With $\delta$, the remaining bias is within about one standard deviation.
  \item The goodness check as first written fails a description known to be correct
  in about half of all cases: a correct description passes only
  \pn{jg.kgnulllo}--\pn{jg.kgnullhi}\% of the time. We therefore compare the check
  with its own null distribution. With $\delta$, the mean field falls inside the
  null's 95\% range at \pn{jg.deltain} targets, against \pn{jg.mfin} without
  $\delta$.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{The mean-field model is the limit of the hybrid.} The hybrid exchanges
state every second. As this step shrinks, the hybrid's mean converges to the
one-ODE mean-field model. At the zero-step limit the two agree to
\pn{rii.maxdelta} in log concentration at all five test parameter sets. One
mean-field solve takes \pn{rii.mzero}\,s on M0 and \pn{rii.corealo}--\pn{rii.coreahi}\,s
on the full reduced model.

With $\delta$ in place, what remains at the level of the posterior is small on
average over datasets, and larger on one dataset for a reason we have not found
(\cref{sec:approx}).

\subsection{The mixed route on M0: calibrated, with small declared discrepancies}
\label{sec:approx}

We refitted the \pn{mix.R} datasets of the exact sampler's calibration with the mixed
route, using \pn{mix.chains} chains of \pn{mix.nadapt} adaptation and \pn{mix.ndraw}
sampling steps per dataset.

\paragraph{Calibration and cost.} The mixed route passes simulation-based calibration
on all \pn{mzero.npar} parameters over the \pn{mix.R} datasets, using the bands of
\cref{sec:exact} (\cref{fig:approx}). The largest deviation is \pn{mix.sbcmax}, for
the transcript decay rate. A posterior costs a median of \pn{mix.cpuh} CPU-hours
(range \pn{mix.cpuhlo}--\pn{mix.cpuhhi}), against \pn{ften.cpuh}--\pn{cost.exacthi}
for the exact sampler. That median counts completed jobs only: a fault in the
starting optimiser lost another \pn{mix.lost} CPU-hours across the whole run.

\paragraph{Discrepancies against the exact posteriors.} Over the \pn{mix.R} datasets,
the largest mean bias against the exact posteriors is \pn{mix.biasmax}~$\pm$~
\pn{mix.biasse} posterior standard deviations, for the decay rate. The median ratio of
standard deviations per parameter is between \pn{mix.sdlo} and \pn{mix.sdhi}. On the
single reference dataset of \cref{sec:exact}, the ptsG promoter, the decay rate and
enolase sit \pn{mix.gaplo}--\pn{mix.gaphi} standard deviations from the exact
reference, about \pn{mix.gapfloor} above the matched noise floor. Re-estimating $S$ at
the posterior mean does not close this gap, and its cause is open. A calibration test
over many datasets does not exclude a gap of this size on one dataset, so we report
both.

\paragraph{Holding $S$ fixed.} $S$ is computed once, at a reference parameter value.
A sensitivity check re-estimated it at the posterior mean on three datasets. The
marginals moved detectably on \pn{mix.ksfired} comparisons, each by at most
\pn{mix.ksmargin} standard deviations over its floor. Against a matched null of two
identical fits, two of the five are about twice the noise, one is 1.3 times it, and
two are within it.

\paragraph{One data stream at a time.} On the reference dataset under transcripts only,
none of the five marginals we compared differs detectably from the exact fit: the
Wasserstein distance is \pn{mix.txlo}--\pn{mix.txhi} exact standard deviations, each
below its noise floor. Under metabolites only, enolase, the decay rate and $\sigb$
differ detectably, by small margins, and aldolase does not. The exact fit has an
effective sample size of only \pn{mix.krnadegess} for the decay rate. Each floor is one sample of the
noise, so these margins are not precise, and we have not tested whether they share a
cause with the gap above.
The mixed route shrinks the ptsG promoter under metabolites only, to \pn{mix.ptsgbulk}
of its prior standard deviation, against \pn{info.mzeroptsg} from the linearised bound.
The exact fit under metabolites only did not mix for this parameter, so this result has
no exact comparison.

\paragraph{Limits.} In \pn{mix.lowconv} of the \pn{mix.R} replicates the two chains
fall short of $\Rhat \le 1.02$ and a bulk effective sample size of 100. The worst
$\Rhat$ is \pn{mix.rhat} and the smallest effective sample size is \pn{mix.ess}. The
calibration result is unchanged without those replicates.

what we know of its effect on $Q$. Only the formalism swap is measured, because only it
has a column in the crossed design. Every other reduction is declared and not bounded, so
the budget does not cover the full uncertainty a modeller should carry.

\paragraph{The one-way coupling on the reduced model.} The elasticities that justify this
reduction hold while GTP stays well above the half-saturation constant of RNA polymerase.
At the default width, \pn{kd.lo}--\pn{kd.hi}\% of baseline cells fall below it at least
once, and \pn{kd.zero}\% reach exactly zero. Clip counts bound the time below it from below:
\pn{kd.boundn} of \pn{budget.ncellsbase} cells spend more than \pn{kd.boundpct}\% of their
post-debit instants at zero. This bound says nothing about time between zero and the
threshold, so it undercounts. An earlier screen, with the inferred parameters drawn from
their priors rather than this posterior, found GTP below the threshold at
\pn{kd.screenlo}--\pn{kd.screenhi}\% of rebuild instants in \pn{kd.screenn} of 20 draws,
and at none in \pn{kd.screenzero}. On those draws the basis does not hold over the full
cycle, whatever the dose. On M0 the same screen found GTP below the threshold at no
instant at the chosen dose. So this caveat applies to the reduced model and not to the
scored system, and the scored check does not cover it.

\subsection{Cost}
\label{sec:cost}

\Cref{tab:cost} gives the cost of one posterior by each route. An exact posterior on
the full reduced model is projected at about \pn{cost.fullscale} CPU-hours, above
our bound of \pn{cost.kone} CPU-hours per posterior. Exact inference therefore
stops at M0. Scoring the reduced model's prediction would need \pn{mzero.cells} such
posteriors. The mixed route costs far less on M0, but we have not calibrated it on the
reduced model. The cost is the number of simulations, not the time of one. One cycle
of the full reduced model takes \pn{model.cyclewall}\,s, and a fast
implementation of the full \syn{} model is the subject of a companion paper
\cite{kimpson2026whole}.
