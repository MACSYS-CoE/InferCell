\section{From posterior to scored prediction}
\label{sec:reducedpredict}

A posterior is worth having if its predictions carry past the data it was fitted to. We ask
how far the joint posterior of \cref{sec:reduced} carries, through \code{predict}
(\cref{sec:predictapi}), to two things the fit never saw. The first is a later horizon: the
saves from \pn{kim95.horizonlo} to \pn{model.cycle}\,s, after a fit window that covers the
first half of the cycle. The second is a knockdown of ptsG, which the data never contain. It
scales the ptsG promoter strength by $1-d$ at dose $d = \pn{kim95.dose}$. We
score both against the true simulator at $\theta^\ast$, with the prior predictive beside the
posterior predictive.

\paragraph{How the predictions are scored.} Every target is a population mean, the mean over a
draw's cells. The truth is the mean over the true simulator's cells at $\theta^\ast$. Three
targets are $Q$, the extracellular lactate at \pn{model.cycle}\,s, that is, the lactate exported over the cycle, in particles (\cref{sec:predictapi}); $Q_{\mathrm{kd}}$, the same
quantity under the knockdown; and their difference $\Delta Q = Q_{\mathrm{kd}} - Q$. The others are the \pn{mzero.npanel} pools and
\pn{kim92.npromoter} transcripts at each of the \pn{kim95.nsaves} saves of the later horizon. Each arm has \pn{kim95.ndraws} parameter draws, with \pn{kim95.cells} cells per
posterior draw and \pn{kim95.priorcells} per prior draw. The truth has \pn{kim95.ntruth} cells
for each knockdown arm. We fixed these sizes before any truth was read. We score each target by the continuous ranked probability score (CRPS) of its predictive
distribution against the truth \cite{gneiting2007strictly}. The CRPS skill is
$1 - \mathrm{CRPS}_{\mathrm{post}}/\mathrm{CRPS}_{\mathrm{prior}}$, so 0 means no better than
the prior. The hit fraction is the share of saves whose true value lies inside the central
50\% or 90\% interval. The Monte Carlo (MC) share is the part of the spread across draws that is cell noise: the mean
over draws of $\hat\sigma^2/n$, one draw's cell variance over its number of cells, divided by the
variance of the draws' means. It can exceed 1 when the spread that comes from $\theta$ is too
small to resolve; the noise-subtracted parameter variance is then negative, and we report the
target as not resolved. Saves within a
species are strongly correlated, so the hit fraction of the pools, or of the transcripts, rests on about \pn{mzero.npanel} species, not on
\pn{kim95.nsavesfam} independent checks. \Cref{sec:reduceddesign} gives the design, and \cref{sec:prediction} the estimand and the
intervention.

\paragraph{$Q$ is covered; the knockdown's effect is not resolved.} The posterior's 90\%
intervals on $Q$ and $Q_{\mathrm{kd}}$ are $\pm\pn{kim95.qint}\%$ wide, and both hold the truth
(\cref{fig:reducedpredict}A). $Q$ is not wholly unseen: the bulk panel includes extracellular lactate, so the fit saw a noisy measure of the same export over the first half of the cycle. The truth sits \pn{kim95.qz} posterior SD below the median on
$Q$ and \pn{kim95.qkdz} SD below it on $Q_{\mathrm{kd}}$, inside both 50\% intervals. The
prior's median is \pn{kim95.priorlow}\% low, and its 90\% interval spans a factor of
\pn{kim95.priorfactor}. The CRPS skill is \pn{kim95.qskill} on both. It is near 1 mostly because the prior is
so wide. The finding is therefore that a narrow interval holds the truth. The knockdown's true effect is
$+\pn{kim95.dqtruth} \pm \pn{kim95.dqse}$, about \pn{kim95.dqshare}\% of $Q$. On this truth the
knockdown slightly raises export, the opposite sign to the fall expected on M0 and in the
budget transferred from M0 (\cref{app:m0predict}). The posterior's 90\% interval on $\Delta Q$
spans zero, so it does not resolve the sign. All of the posterior's width on
$\Delta Q$ is cell noise (MC share \pn{kim95.dqmc}). The posterior therefore shows only that
the effect is small next to one draw's cell noise. Because the effect is this small, covering
$Q_{\mathrm{kd}}$ tests nearly the same thing as covering $Q$. The CRPS skill on $\Delta Q$ is
\pn{kim95.dqskill}, against a prior whose median is negative, the opposite sign to the truth. The truth is also less
sharp than the sizing rule asks. On these three targets its standard error is
\pn{kim95.sesdlo}--\pn{kim95.sesdhi} of the posterior SD, above the rule's \pn{kim95.sesdrule}.
The posterior is narrower than the sizing passes, run before the full prediction
(\cref{sec:reduceddesign}), could see. These hits are therefore read against a truth that is
not sharp.

\paragraph{The transcripts are covered, but mostly by cell noise.} On the later horizon,
\pn{kim95.txin} of the \pn{kim95.nsavesfam} transcript saves lie inside the posterior's 90\%
interval, and \pn{kim95.txin50}\% inside its 50\% interval. For the prior these fractions are
\pn{kim95.txprior90}\% and \pn{kim95.txprior50}\%. We define a species' offset as the median over its saves of the truth minus the
posterior median, in posterior SD, so a positive offset means the truth lies above the
prediction. Every transcript's offset is within \pn{kim95.txzmax} SD
(\cref{fig:reducedpredict}B). The transcripts' median MC share is \pn{kim95.txmc}, so about
two-thirds of their predictive variance is cell noise. Their hit fractions therefore overstate
how sharp the parameter part of the prediction is.

\paragraph{The pools show where the posterior is wrong.} \pn{kim95.poolin90}\% of pool saves
lie inside the posterior's 90\% interval, but only \pn{kim95.poolin50}\% inside its 50\%
interval, against \pn{kim95.poolprior90}\% and \pn{kim95.poolprior50}\% for the prior. For
\pn{kim95.poolshift} of the \pn{mzero.npanel} pools the shortfall is a shift and not scatter. Their offsets are
about \pn{kim95.shiftlo}--\pn{kim95.shifthi} SD in magnitude. In the trioses the truth lies above
the prediction, by \pn{kim95.g3p} SD for G3P and \pn{kim95.dhap} SD for DHAP, and no save of
either falls inside its 50\% interval (\cref{fig:reducedpredict}C). The energy charge splits,
with the truth below the prediction for ATP, ADP and GDP and above it for AMP and GMP. In upper
glycolysis the truth also lies about one SD above the prediction for G6P, F6P and FDP. The other \pn{kim95.poolsquiet}
pools sit within \pn{kim95.quietz} SD. The triose and FDP offsets come from $\theta$ and not from cell
noise. Their median MC shares are \pn{kim95.triosemclo}--\pn{kim95.triosemchi}, so these offsets
come from the posterior's $\theta$; on one dataset they may still reflect this data realisation. The nucleotides' shares are larger, \pn{kim95.nucmclo}--\pn{kim95.nucmchi}. The median CRPS skill over
all species is still \pn{kim95.medskill}. In \cref{sec:reduced} the posterior means of the promoter
strengths sit below the truth, with a mean standardised error of \pn{kim92.promz}, yet the
transcripts show no comparable shift. Scoring against the true simulator tests the mixed route's
approximation on the reduced model. By that test, on this truth, the posterior's error shows in the pools and
not in $Q$ or the transcripts.

\paragraph{Once every parameter is inferred, $Q$'s uncertainty comes from the data.} By the
contraction threshold of \cref{sec:reduced}, the data reach all \pn{kim92.ncoord} parameters,
and the lowest contraction is \pn{kim92.contrmin}. No parameter stays prior-dominated, so there is no held set
whose share of the variance could be measured. Instead we compare $Q$'s parameter variance
$\mathrm{Var}_\theta(Q)$, in particles$^2$: its variance across parameter draws with the cell
noise taken out, written $\mathrm{Var}(\mu)$ in \cref{app:m0predict}. We compare it in three
states, all with the promoters' priors at the default width, a geometric SD of
\pn{budget.gsdone} (\cref{fig:reducedbudget}). With all \pn{kim92.nmodelpar} model parameters at
their priors ($\sigb$, the assay noise, does not enter $Q$), $\mathrm{Var}_\theta(Q)$ is \pn{kim96.vprior}. The middle state, the budget transferred from M0, puts M0's
posterior on \pn{budget.nI} parameters and the \pn{budget.nH} other promoter strengths at their
priors. There it is \pn{kim96.vkimfortytwo}, \pn{kim96.heldshare}\% of it from those promoters
(\cref{app:m0predict}). Under the joint posterior it is \pn{kim96.vpost}. As a standard
deviation relative to each state's own mean $Q$, the three values are \pn{kim96.sdprior}\%,
\pn{kim96.sdkimfortytwo}\% and \pn{kim96.sdpost}\%. The joint
posterior's variance is about \pn{kim96.ratio} times below the middle state's
(\pn{kim96.ratiolo}--\pn{kim96.ratiohi}, $\pm 2$ standard errors on the log scale) and about
\pn{kim96.priorratio} times below the prior's.

\paragraph{What the factor of \pn{kim96.ratio} means.} The factor holds at the default width
only. At widths $\tfrac12$ and 2 the middle state changes, and the factor is about
\pn{kim96.widthlo} and \pn{kim96.widthhi}. Nor does the factor come from the promoters alone. Pinning the \pn{budget.nH} promoters exactly while
keeping M0's posterior on the other \pn{budget.nI} would leave \pn{kim96.hwithin}, a factor of
at most about \pn{kim96.honly}. The rest comes from the reduced model's data also tightening those
\pn{budget.nI}, and the joint posterior does both at once. The three states also have different
centres: the middle state's mean $Q$ is \pn{budget.qone} million, the joint posterior's
\pn{kim96.meanpost} million. The ratios therefore compare variances about different means,
and they are not a decomposition of one distribution. Last, the data came from this same model at
$\theta^\ast$. The \pn{kim96.sdpost}\% is precision under a correct model, and the pools above
show that it is not a calibrated uncertainty on every output.
