\begin{abstract}
Whole-cell models fix each parameter at one value, so their predictions carry no error bar for
parameter uncertainty. Inference is hard because metabolism runs as ordinary differential
equations (ODEs) and gene expression as a stochastic jump process with latent paths. Here we
present \IC{}, a compositional inference framework, and test it on one synthetic dataset from a
reduced whole-cell model of \syn{}, whose gene expression is first order. Its likelihood spans
the coupled ODE--stochastic boundary. An exact sampler is paired with a mixed route that
averages over the latent paths. On M0, a two-gene slice of the model, both routes pass a
low-powered simulation-based calibration, but on one dataset they differ by
\pn{mix.gaplo}--\pn{mix.gaphi} standard deviations for an unknown reason. The mixed route costs
about \pn{mix.speedup} times less than an exact production chain. On the reduced model, it gives
one joint posterior over all \pn{kim92.ncoord} free parameters for about \pn{kim92.cpuh}
CPU-hours. The data narrow every parameter and the posterior reproduces its own data, but the
promoter strengths sit about \pn{kim92.promshift}\% low. Neither of two two-stage alternatives,
as designed, is both cheaper and faithful: one lands close to the joint posterior at
\pn{kim116.cutt1} times its cost, and a cheaper one shifts the promoters and predicts lactate
worse. Scored against the true simulator on a later horizon and under a ptsG knockdown, the
predictions cover extracellular lactate at the end of the cycle and the transcripts, do not
resolve the knockdown's small effect, and miss \pn{kim95.poolshift} of the \pn{mzero.npanel}
metabolite pools by about \pn{kim95.shiftlo}--\pn{kim95.shifthi} standard deviations. On this
test, a posterior on a reduced whole-cell model can be carried to scored predictions that show
where it is wrong, and each score names the declared reductions it does not cover.
\end{abstract}
