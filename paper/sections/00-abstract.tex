\begin{abstract}
Whole-cell models fix each parameter at one value, so their predictions carry no error bar.
Inference is hard because metabolism runs as ordinary differential equations (ODEs) and gene
expression as a stochastic jump process with latent paths. Here we present \IC{}, a compositional
inference framework, and use it to infer a reduced whole-cell model of \syn{} across a
coupled ODE--stochastic boundary and to turn the posterior into predictions scored against
held-out truth, on synthetic data from one truth.
An exact sampler is paired with a mixed route that integrates out the paths. On M0, a two-gene slice of the model, both routes pass simulation-based calibration,
and the mixed route costs about \pn{mix.speedup} times less. On the reduced model, the mixed
route gives one joint posterior over all 21 free parameters for about \pn{kim92.cpuh}
CPU-hours; the data reach every parameter, and the posterior reproduces its own data. Neither of
two two-stage alternatives, as designed, was both cheaper and faithful: a fit that cuts the
bulk data's feedback on the promoters lands close to the joint posterior at \pn{kim116.cutt1}
times its cost, and a cheaper one that reads the rate constants from the observed pools
shifts the promoters and predicts lactate worse. Scored
against the true simulator on a later horizon and under a ptsG knockdown, the predictions
cover extracellular lactate and the transcripts, do not resolve the knockdown's small effect,
and miss \pn{kim95.poolshift} of 17 metabolite pools by about \pn{kim95.shiftlo}--\pn{kim95.shifthi} standard deviations. At the
default prior width, lactate's parameter variance is about \pn{kim96.ratio} times below the
budget transferred from M0.
A whole-cell posterior can thus be carried to scored predictions that show where it is
wrong. Because every departure from the published model is declared, each score states what
it does not cover.
\end{abstract}
