\begin{abstract}
Whole-cell models simulate a cell from its molecular parts but fix each parameter at one
value, so their predictions carry no error bar. Inference is hard because metabolism runs as
ordinary differential equations (ODEs) and gene expression as a stochastic jump process with
latent paths, and no parameter links the two. Here we present \IC{}, a compositional
inference framework, and use it to infer a reduced whole-cell model of \syn{} across a
coupled ODE--stochastic boundary and to turn the posterior into predictions scored against
held-out truth.
The framework pairs an exact sampler with a mixed route that integrates out the stochastic
paths. On M0, a two-gene slice of the model, both routes pass simulation-based calibration,
and the mixed route costs about \pn{mix.speedup} times less. On the reduced model, the mixed
route gives one joint posterior over all 21 free parameters for about \pn{kim92.cpuh}
CPU-hours; the data reach every parameter, and the posterior reproduces its own data. Scored
against the true simulator on a later horizon and under a ptsG knockdown, the predictions
cover extracellular lactate and the transcripts, do not resolve the knockdown's small effect, and miss \pn{kim95.poolshift} of 17
metabolite pools by about \pn{kim95.shiftlo}--\pn{kim95.shifthi} standard deviations. At the
default prior width, lactate's parameter variance is about \pn{kim96.ratio} times below the
budget transferred from M0. The data are synthetic, from one truth.
A whole-cell posterior can thus be carried to scored predictions that show where it is
wrong. Because every departure from the published model is declared, each score states what
it does not cover.
\end{abstract}
